# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the single source-confirmed fact and let the rest of the item orbit it.
- Keep headline and intro aligned on the same core claim, but have the lede add something new rather than restating the headline.
- Use a tight, grounded update when the source is thin; restraint beats expansion.
- Let a small update stay small instead of forcing broad implications or analysis.
- Build the first sentence to carry the actual news, especially on simple update items.
- Use names, dates, venue details, or figures only when they sharpen the angle, not as filler.
- Favor clean finished-report tone over source-meta scaffolding or narration.
- Keep the opening crisp and immediate on tight update items.
- Stay inside the source digest when the story’s value is just one confirmed takeaway.
- [analyst] When a historical benchmark is relevant, use it as secondary context only after the main current development is clearly anchored.
- [insider] Tight, team-specific updates work when they stay grounded in the confirmed move plus any directly stated context.
- When the source gives a real news hook, pair it with the most relevant emotional detail without losing grounding.
- [analyst] Strong historical context can boost specificity when it stays clearly subordinate to the present-tense update.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a narrow update into broader team, contract, or roster context unless the source truly supports it.
- Don’t repeat the headline’s wording in the lede without adding a new fact or angle.
- Don’t bury the one concrete fact under generic expansion language or background.
- Don’t mismatch the headline, subhead, and lede by making each one say the same thing.
- Don’t inflate a tentative or thin source into a bigger consequence story than the evidence supports.
- Don’t lean on source-narration phrasing or visible scaffolding in final prose.
- Don’t pad a usable angle with extra specifics the digest doesn’t provide.
- Don’t substitute broad commentary for the exact confirmed detail that makes the item newsworthy.
- Don’t overbuild a simple report; sparse updates should stay literal and limited.
- Don’t make a benchmark comparison sound like an imminent real-world plan unless the source actually says that.
- Don’t introduce unsupported spin about implications, especially when the source offers no concrete follow-through.
- Don’t publish or rewrite from a missing digest; if there are no source facts to validate against, the piece is source-incomplete and not safely fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t assign teams or other entities that aren’t in the source material; unsupported framing makes the piece ungrounded.
- [analyst] Don’t let a historical pairing or milestone eclipse the present-tense update; keep the current claim centered first.
- [insider] Don’t broaden a narrow confirmation into off-digest narrative beats; keep it on the verified point and any directly stated additional detail.
