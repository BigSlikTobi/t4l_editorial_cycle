# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the specific verified camp decision immediately when the source gives a clear workload or role change.
- Keep headline and lede aligned on the same confirmed takeaway, but make each do distinct work.
- For analyst pieces, add a sharper team-specific angle instead of stopping at a generic status recap.
- Use the headline/subhead to surface the real contest or evaluation window when the source supports it.
- Trim repetitive phrasing so the lede advances beyond the headline instead of echoing it.
- Keep the story grounded in the source-digest claim while sharpening the editorial angle around it.
- Use restrained, direct wording for camp-management or quarterback-evaluation items.
- When the source has enough substance, turn it into a concrete takeaway rather than a bland summary.
- Preserve clarity over flourish, but avoid flattening the piece into a near-paraphrase of the source.
- When the source is well-supported, make the competition or workload implication explicit without overexplaining.

## What to avoid
- Do not restate the same sentence in multiple forms; the lede must add new value.
- Do not leave an analyst story at a generic, source-digest level when there is a sharper team-specific takeaway available.
- Do not underuse the headline/subhead; they should highlight the main conflict, evaluation, or decision, not repeat a bland summary.
- Do not make the piece feel mechanically grounded but editorially thin.
- Do not hide the real angle by focusing only on the broad status update.
- Do not let repetitive phrasing dilute the actual news.
- Do not keep the copy so cautious that it misses the controlled-workload or competition angle.
- Do not flatten a meaningful camp decision into a generic recap.
- Do not overbuild context beyond the source, but also do not settle for a bare restatement.
- Do not infer a pecking order or other unsupported detail when the source does not actually provide usable facts (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] Do not write as if the only job is to summarize; the analyst persona should surface the actionable takeaway from the source facts.
- [analyst] Do not rely on unsupported inferential language to bridge gaps in the source.
