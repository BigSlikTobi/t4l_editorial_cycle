# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the exact verified fact when the digest gives a clean, specific breaking-news angle.
- Keep the headline tightly aligned with the body’s central claim; a clean Stafford-style angle works when it matches the sourced update.
- Use a direct lede for insider/breaking pieces when the key news is already obvious and specific.
- Stay tightly grounded in the supplied source facts; strong articles usually come from resisting embellishment.
- Use precise numbers, labels, and timing when they are confirmed rather than generalizing them.
- Keep the piece anchored on one primary news hook, even if a secondary detail is available.
- Match the persona’s job: deliver the core fact cleanly, fast, and without extra narrative weight.
- Preserve a simple, factual read when the source package is complete and unambiguous.
- [analyst] You can widen slightly into team-specific implications if they stay clearly tied to verified source facts.
- When the source is solid, concise repetition of the same verified point can reinforce the central news without overcomplicating it.

## What to avoid
- Don’t let the headline drift away from the actual verified angle in the body.
- Don’t write a generic lede when the source gives a sharper, cleaner news hook.
- Don’t widen a source-backed fact into a broader thesis unless the digest clearly supports it.
- Don’t over-explain a straightforward breaking item with extra context that adds no new verified value.
- Don’t pile on speculation, color, or interpretation when the digest already supplies the full news claim.
- Don’t blur a precise deal/story into a vague “major move” summary that loses the core numbers or status.
- Don’t add unsupported quotes, stats, or team/context details.
- Don’t force a broader analytical frame onto a piece that works best as a concise, source-locked update.
- [analyst] Don’t let the draft’s supporting paragraph outrun the core update; if you widen, keep the expansion tightly tethered to the verified facts.
- Don’t assert facts that aren’t clearly present in the digest; if the source is thin or mismatched, treat it as a source problem, not a prose fix (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t ignore metadata or entity mismatches if the digest doesn’t cleanly support the claim; avoid manufacturing a news peg from unusable source material (source-side, not fixable in prose).
