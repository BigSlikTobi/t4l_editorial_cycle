# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the one source-backed fact or quote when the digest is narrow.
- Keep the lede and headline built from the same limited fact set.
- Use a restrained, source-faithful frame when there is only one clear development to report.
- Preserve a clean, direct setup instead of padding a thin digest with extra context.
- Stay tightly grounded when source support is modest; compact can be stronger than expansive.
- Clear, concrete hooks from the source can carry a piece even without much added color.
- High grounding can outweigh middling impact if every claim stays inside the digest.
- Strong headline/lede alignment remains a reliable approve signal across personas.
- [insider] When the digest truly supports it, name the person, organization, role effect, and timing plainly, but keep the rest of the piece restrained.

## What to avoid
- Do not add role, status, or background specifics unless the digest explicitly supports them.
- Do not inflate a thin summary into a fuller story by assuming unsupported contract or roster details.
- Do not let the headline introduce facts that the lede cannot safely support.
- Do not widen a narrow digest with extra analysis just to create movement.
- Do not overstate certainty when the source only offers cautious or limited language.
- Do not build the piece around unsupported status or role framing if the digest does not confirm it.
- Do not treat a good angle as a substitute for closed-world grounding.
- Do not reach beyond the digest for details that sound plausible but are not sourced.
- [insider] Do not publish when the digest has no usable key facts; unsupported role/timing claims are a hard grounding failure.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the digest lacks solid facts entirely, the problem is source substance, not just execution.
- [columnist] Avoid turning a single quoted sentiment into a substantive narrative when the digest supplies no additional facts; keep the treatment minimal or pass.
- [columnist] Do not write as if contract/status details are known when the source only supports a headline-level claim.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the only support is a sentiment with no underlying facts, the failure is the source material, not just the drafting.
