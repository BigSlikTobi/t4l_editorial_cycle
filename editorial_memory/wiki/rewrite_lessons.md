# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest source-backed roster move immediately when the digest is narrow.
- Keep the headline and lede aligned on the same limited fact set.
- Preserve required hedges and status nuance instead of flattening them.
- Include only the rehab/timeline context the source actually supports.
- Stay tightly grounded; a compact read can be stronger than an expanded one.
- Use a restrained, source-faithful frame when there is only one central development to report.
- Strong grounding can outweigh moderate impact when every claim stays inside the digest.
- Clean headline/lede alignment is still a reliable approve signal across personas.
- [insider] State the roster move and the confirmed signing plainly, with no extra color, when the digest supports both.
- [insider] Direct, sparse, useful wording is the right fit for clear team transactions with named incoming and outgoing pieces.

## What to avoid
- Do not add injury specifics, role, status, or background details unless the digest explicitly supports them.
- Do not inflate a thin summary into a fuller story by assuming unsupported contract, roster, or medical details.
- Do not let the headline introduce facts that the lede cannot safely support.
- Do not widen a narrow digest with extra analysis just to create movement.
- Do not overstate certainty when the source language is limited or hedged.
- Do not build the piece around unsupported status framing if the digest does not confirm it.
- Do not reach beyond the digest for details that sound plausible but are not sourced.
- Do not replace a concrete source-backed update with source-meta language or extra embellishment.
- Do not repeat the same point in the lede and body in slightly different words; tighten to a clean breaking-news note.
- [insider] Do not publish unsupported medical or timeline claims; if the digest has no usable key facts, that is a hard grounding failure.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the digest lacks solid facts entirely, the problem is source substance, not just execution.
- [columnist] Avoid turning a single quoted sentiment into a substantive narrative when the digest supplies no additional facts; keep the treatment minimal or pass.
