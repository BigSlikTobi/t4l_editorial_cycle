# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the single supported development when it is the clearest news value.
- Keep headline, intro, and first paragraph tightly centered on the same verified update.
- Use a concise, answer-first lede when the digest gives one strong fact.
- Stay inside the provided material; a narrow, fact-bounded update can be enough.
- Preserve the clean payoff for the reader instead of widening into explanation or analysis.
- Strong grounding can offset middling execution when the core development is clear and relevant.
- Match the insider voice by stating the confirmed fact directly, without source-meta language.
- Keep related-name mentions subordinate and thin unless the digest gives them real support.
- When source coverage is thin but real, keep the story tightly scoped and avoid overbuilding it.

## What to avoid
- Do not add source-narration phrasing like “according to sources” when the article can state the fact directly.
- Do not let headline, intro, and opening paragraph drift into separate versions of the same update.
- Do not import extra names or references that are not supported by the digest.
- Do not turn a single injury or transaction note into broader team-direction or roster-planning analysis.
- Do not frame a reported fact with unsupported interpretive labels or implications.
- Do not bury the main update behind setup or meta commentary.
- Do not expand the cast, context, or consequences beyond what the reporting actually says.
- [insider] Do not reach for explanatory context outside the digest; keep the piece inside the confirmed material.
- [insider] If the source only validates one injury development, keep the scope on that development rather than adding adjacent personnel.
- [insider] Don’t assert a specific injury status as confirmed when the provided digest does not actually establish it.
- [insider] Don’t keep rewriting around a grounding gap; if the digest has no factual content, the problem is source-side, not fixable in prose.
