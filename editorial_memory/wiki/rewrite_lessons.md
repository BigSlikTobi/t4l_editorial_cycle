# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the confirmed transaction or development immediately; make the first sentence carry the news.
- Keep the story tightly anchored to the one source-confirmed core fact when the digest is thin.
- Let a narrow update stay narrow instead of forcing broader team, contract, or roster implications.
- Keep headline and lede aligned on the same core claim, but add a real new detail in the opening sentence.
- Use a clean finished-report tone rather than source-meta scaffolding or narration.
- Keep wording specific and grounded when the source gives a usable, direct update.
- Use names, dates, venue details, or figures only when they sharpen the angle, not as filler.
- [analyst] When a historical benchmark is relevant, use it as secondary context only after the present-day claim is clearly centered.
- [insider] Tight roster-development updates work best when they stay on the verified move plus any directly stated context.
- [insider] Clear, specific transaction framing lands when it avoids unsupported claims and stays inside the digest.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a narrow update into broader team, contract, or roster context unless the source truly supports it.
- Don’t repeat the headline’s wording in the lede without adding a new fact or angle.
- Don’t bury the one concrete fact under generic expansion language or background.
- Don’t lean on source-meta phrasing like “according to the report” or “no further terms were included” unless the digest explicitly supports it.
- Don’t mismatch the headline, subhead, and lede by making each one say the same thing.
- Don’t inflate a tentative or thin source into a bigger consequence story than the evidence supports.
- Don’t pad a usable angle with extra specifics the digest doesn’t provide.
- Don’t substitute broad commentary for the exact confirmed detail that makes the item newsworthy.
- Don’t overbuild a simple report; sparse updates should stay literal and limited.
- Don’t introduce unsupported timing, contract, or follow-through details just to flesh out a short source.
- Don’t publish or rewrite from a missing digest; if there are no source facts to validate against, the piece is source-incomplete and not safely fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t broaden a narrow confirmation into off-digest narrative beats; keep it on the verified point and any directly stated additional detail.
- Don’t invent a story when the digest is missing or empty; unsupported claims about a move or competition are source-incomplete and not safely salvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
