# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open with the clearest verified news peg and keep the headline and lede aligned on the same concrete fact.
- Lead with the main person/event first; don’t bury the central update under extra framing.
- Keep pieces disciplined when the source is narrow: a simple, source-backed update can work if every sentence stays inside verified facts.
- Tight packages work best when the article is built around one confirmed development rather than padded with side context.
- Clean execution often comes from stripping away meta language and letting the supported update stand on its own.
- A specific, source-grounded headline and intro can still create a tap reason if they stay inside the facts.
- High-specificity sourcing can carry a narrow piece when the article avoids overreaching beyond the digest.
- Use concise status/outcome phrasing when the source only confirms a basic fact pattern.
- Cautious, source-true injury or condition language can sharpen relevance when it stays limited to what the report actually says.
- [insider] Fast, factual, and specific execution fits the persona well when it adds only grounded details.
- [insider] Clean summary structures and concrete names/statuses can deliver a strong update when the digest supports it.
- [insider] When two verified developments are available, carry both through the headline and lede for better relevance and grounding.

## What to avoid
- Do not add unverified medical or causal detail when the digest is effectively empty; keep the lede to what is actually confirmed.
- Do not package a confirmed news peg with extra claims about what else did or did not happen unless the source explicitly says so.
- Do not let headline, lede, and body drift into different levels of certainty; the opening stack should reinforce one verified development.
- Do not repeat the same core fact in multiple forms when the fact set is thin; tighten instead of paraphrasing.
- Do not infer broader team-impact, legacy, or narrative consequences from a limited digest unless the source supports them.
- Do not pad a narrow story with source-meta filler or commentary about the digest itself.
- Do not introduce unsupported timing, secondary details, or storyline consequences beyond the confirmed update.
- [insider] Do not broaden a basic update into a more complete story than the source supports; keep the language factual and bounded.
- [insider] Avoid unsupported color; specificity should come from verified details, not inference.
- [insider] If the source digest is missing supporting content, do not force a rewrite into a fuller story; treat it as a grounding failure (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Remove roster-context or background paragraphs when the source digest is effectively empty; keep the piece on the single verified move or event.
- [insider] Avoid unsupported player references or role labels when they are not directly supported by the source digests.
- [insider] When the source package contains two linked developments, include both in the headline and lede instead of treating the story as a single-team update.
- [insider] If the source package is solid but the draft pulls in extra names/details outside the closed world, do a clean rewrite and strip them out.
- [columnist] If the source record has no usable facts, do not invent specifics to salvage the story; this is a grounding failure (source-side, not fixable in prose).
