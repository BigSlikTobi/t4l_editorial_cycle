# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the concrete change immediately, especially when the source supplies a clear role shift, status update, or transaction.
- Keep the headline and lede on the same specific fact; narrow analyst items work best when both layers say exactly what changed.
- Use the source’s strongest detail as the opening hook instead of circling a broad premise.
- Trim repetitive paraphrase; a concise lead feels sharper than re-saying the same idea in multiple ways.
- When the source is limited, stay tightly inside the verified wrinkle rather than stretching for extra analysis.
- Source-anchored specificity can carry a small update even without a big news event, as long as the writing stays precise.
- Clean, direct framing helps analyst pieces read as updates rather than vague commentary.
- Tight openings work best when they identify the real movement in the story, not just the general topic.

## What to avoid
- Do not leave the reader with only a broad premise when the source supports a concrete development.
- Do not pad analyst copy with repeated flexibility/uncertainty language if the key fact is already clear.
- Do not let the intro drift into generalities when a more specific source-backed wrinkle is available.
- Do not build a lead out of paraphrase loops; each sentence should add something new.
- Do not overexplain a small role-change item with extra analytical weight that the source does not support.
- Do not mismatch the headline and lede by making the headline more specific than the body.
- Do not widen a narrow source fact into larger implications unless the digest actually supplies them.
- [analyst] Do not treat a role-adjustment note as a springboard for abstract scheme talk when the source only confirms movement and usage.
- [analyst] Do not write as if flexibility itself is the story; show the concrete on-field change the source gives you.
- Do not let a thin source package feel vague because the strongest detail was buried instead of surfaced.
- Do not build around broad paraphrase when the source digest is missing or unusable; without verifiable facts, the item is too undergrounded to publish (source-side, not fixable in prose).
