# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open with the clearest verified development and keep the headline, lede, and intro aligned on the same concrete takeaway.
- Lead with the most important confirmed angle first — status, decision, timeline, or role — before any broader framing.
- Keep the story tightly inside the digest; a narrow, source-backed update can still work when it stays disciplined.
- Let one concrete hook or quote do the heavy lifting when the source is brief or limited.
- Use short, direct status or outcome phrasing when the facts are specific and limited.
- When the source supplies a clean sequence of events, present that sequence plainly instead of overexplaining it.
- Tight packages work best when every sentence advances the verified update or the most relevant sourced consequence.
- Clean execution often comes from stripping away meta language and keeping only the supported update.
- A specific, source-grounded headline and intro can create a tap reason on a narrow update as long as they stay within confirmed facts.
- [analyst] Temporary role changes can work when the article stays centered on the concrete week-to-week move and keeps the framing tight.
- [analyst] When a source only suggests a role or depth status, keep the wording tentative and build around the named options rather than overstating a settled plan.

## What to avoid
- Do not package two unrelated stories as one lead; split them cleanly so the opening has a single sharp focus.
- Do not let headline, subhead, and intro pull in different directions; the opening stack should reinforce one concrete development first.
- Do not repeat the same core thought across headline, lede, and body; if the fact is thin, tighten the package instead of paraphrasing it three times.
- Do not rely on a mixed breaking-news bundle when the piece needs a single dominant angle to feel tap-worthy.
- Do not publish or rewrite around an effectively empty digest; unsupported game/player/usage claims are source-side, not fixable in prose.
- Do not treat a plausible headline as evidence when the source text does not verify it.
- Do not imply an “update,” surprise, or meaningful new development when the digest has no usable facts to support that angle.
- Do not infer offensive usage, player involvement, or other closed-world facts from a thin or empty digest.
- Do not add unsupported medical, roster, depth-chart, or matchup detail beyond what the digests confirm.
- Do not overstate a suggestion as a confirmation; if the source only indicates a likely or possible role, keep the lede and headline tentative.
- Do not add source-meta phrasing or filler; cut “reported”/similar framing and any padding when the update can stand on the fact itself.
- [analyst] Do not write a placeholder-style update that merely restates the same temporary move; if the fact is thin, cut repetition and keep the temporary nature of the move as the only real angle.
- [analyst] Do not frame a tentative depth note as a locked-in backup plan; soften the language until the source clearly supports certainty.
- Do not invent supporting context like position labels or roster-activation explanations when the digest is empty or vague; if the source has no facts, the draft is not recoverable (source-side, not fixable in prose).
