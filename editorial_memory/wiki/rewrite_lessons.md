# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the single confirmed transaction or status change when that is the only grounded news; a clear signing, extension, injury update, or roster move can carry the piece.
- Keep the lede tightly centered on the one verified fact instead of stretching into extra context or speculation.
- A source-light update can still publish well if the opening is crisp, factual, and anchored in the exact reported move.
- Keep the headline aligned with the confirmed takeaway; one clean hook is enough.
- Build around concrete source-backed facts rather than broadening into significance or narrative.
- Preserve uncertainty when the source does not support extra timing, outcome, or contextual claims.
- Strong grounding can offset moderate scores elsewhere if the article never drifts beyond the confirmed fact.
- Keep team-specific updates direct and answer-oriented when they give readers a real roster or contract answer. [insider]
- [insider] When a roster move is the news, make the move and the reason for it the headline/lede spine.
- [insider] Include only confirmed deal or status details that materially change the update; do not pad with unrelated context.
- [insider] A tight, source-backed anchor detail can help, but only when it is explicitly reported.
- [insider] Clean, specific prose still matters, but it cannot rescue an article if the underlying source digests do not validate the facts.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into broader narrative, leaguewide significance, or contract-market implications without support.
- Do not let the headline or intro promise certainty the source does not provide.
- Do not add unsupported player, teammate, practice-rep, or depth-chart references when the source only confirms the top-line transaction.
- Do not add unsupported timing, outcome, timetable, or decision certainty beyond the confirmed facts.
- Do not mix in extra context just to make a short update feel bigger than it is.
- Do not repeat the same short update across headline, subhead, and intro; compress cleanly and move only useful extras deeper in the piece.
- Do not lean on process language, structural explanations, or behind-the-scenes commentary unless the source explicitly supports it.
- Do not turn a bare or source-light story into a columnist-style take; if the digest has no extractable facts, the framing is not salvageable in prose.
- Do not treat source-heavy-but-digest-light material as permission to invent context or fill gaps with assumptions.
- [insider] Do not imply broader quarterback or camp consequences from a contract/availability update unless the source directly says so.
- [insider] Do not use a team transaction to smuggle in unsupported roster or depth-chart conclusions.
- [insider] Do not leave repetitive source-summary phrasing in place when a tighter news angle is available.
- [insider] Do not name specific surrounding players or scenarios unless those details appear in the source digests; otherwise treat them as source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [insider] Do not force a rewrite when the source digest is effectively empty; dismiss instead of stretching around unsupported framing.
- [insider] If the provided source digests cannot validate the core reported fact, the issue is source-side, not a prose fix.
