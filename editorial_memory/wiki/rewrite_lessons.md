# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the clearest confirmed development first; a single grounded fact can carry a thin update.
- Keep the headline and lede aligned with the same verified event.
- Lead with the concrete action or outcome, not with editorial framing.
- Use the most specific available source detail to anchor a short update.
- Stay tight when the story only supports one development; brevity is a strength if it is focused.
- Preserve a factual, source-bound tone when no extra context is available.
- Let the confirmed status change or transaction do the work rather than inflating it with commentary.
- Keep insider copy clean and direct when the only support is a confirmed move or availability update.
- [insider] A strong reported quote or figure can supply the needed specificity in a sparse digest.
- [insider] A precise, headline-level transaction or extension can publish cleanly if the intro stays tightly on that fact.

## What to avoid
- Do not repeat the headline almost verbatim in the lede; add new information or a cleaner angle.
- Do not pad a thin update with vague filler or generic “more on the story” language.
- Do not bury the only confirmed development under setup or meta language.
- Do not widen a single confirmed note into speculation about broader team plans or roster implications.
- Do not add unsupported context just to make the story feel fuller.
- Do not use source-report language that weakens the directness of a clean, grounded update.
- Do not over-explain why the piece is short; keep the prose focused on the confirmed fact.
- Do not force analysis onto a report that only supports a factual transaction or status item.
- [insider] Avoid vague editorial scaffolding when the source only gives one concrete update.
- [insider] If no further details are reported, say that plainly instead of dressing it up.
- [insider] Do not add claims about a player’s role or a team’s roster plan unless the digest explicitly supports them.
- [insider] Do not rely on headline/summary alone when the source file does not supply the underlying facts; unsupported grounding is a hard failure.
- [insider] Avoid banned source-narration or meta phrasing that calls attention to the reporting process instead of the update itself.
- [insider] Fix source/team mismatches before anything else; a clean rewrite cannot rescue a story whose metadata points to the wrong team or player (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Cut phrases like “according to the report” and “no other terms were included” when they add meta clutter instead of substance.
- [insider] Do not attempt a rewrite when the source digest is empty or missing key facts; that is a source-side, not fixable in prose issue.
- [insider] Treat missing source content as an unrecoverable grounding failure, especially when the draft repeats unsupported details.
