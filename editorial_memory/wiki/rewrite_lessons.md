# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the single concrete development when the story has one clear payoff.
- Keep breaking-news notes tight, factual, and centered on one verified update.
- Use a clean, direct lede for simple insider items instead of source-meta framing.
- Let a single real development carry the item without filler or report-about-the-report language.
- Preserve strong grounding by staying inside the verified core fact and nothing more.
- Keep headline and lede aligned on the same confirmed angle.
- When the news is narrow, a compact opening can be enough if it is fully supported.
- [insider] Center the piece on the one development the digest actually supports.
- [insider] Clean direct reporting can outperform extra framing when the payoff is a simple status, penalty, or fine update.
- [analyst] Use available numbers, coach quotes, and game details to support the angle without drifting into filler.
- [analyst] A grounded story still reads better when the structure is unified around one tapworthy reason to read.

## What to avoid
- Do not lead with source-meta phrasing like “according to the report” when the fact can be stated directly.
- Avoid repeating the report itself or explaining that it lacks more detail; cut filler about source quality.
- Do not soften a simple breaking update with indirect setup or hedging language.
- Avoid padding a narrow insider note with extra sentences that do not add new verified information.
- Do not bury the key development behind generic framing.
- Avoid turning a clean news item into commentary on the source instead of the fact.
- Do not widen one confirmed update into broader implications the digest does not support.
- Do not let the headline and opening drift away from the single verified angle.
- Avoid unsupported certainty about practice outcomes or who “stood out” when the digest only gives names and context.
- [insider] Avoid source-meta language in the lede; state the update plainly and move on.
- [insider] Do not add filler lines about what the report does not include when the story already has its core fact.
- [insider] Don’t add team-planning or short-term-impact claims beyond a narrowly confirmed injury/timeline or penalty update.
- [analyst] Don’t try to make two separate stories do one headline; split the angle or drop the secondary thread from the lede and body.
- [analyst] Avoid combining unrelated developments in the opening when each would compete for the hook.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Do not try to salvage a piece when the source digest contains no usable facts; a title-level restatement cannot be improved within closed-world constraints.
- [columnist] Do not invent spotlight framing or named-player implications when the digest is effectively empty; unsupported specifics are a hard stop.
- [columnist] If grounding is zero, avoid writing as though the practice note has a verified angle to develop.
- [columnist] Do not add invented practice context or extra participants when the digest only supports the headline-level summary.
- [insider] If the source material is empty or only title-level, do not introduce timeline, injury, or status specifics that were not actually verified.
- [insider] When grounding is near-zero, treat the item as non-publishable rather than trying to force a clean rewrite.
