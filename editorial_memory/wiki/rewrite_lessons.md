# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest supported development when it carries the news value.
- Keep headline, intro, and first paragraph tightly aligned on the same verified update.
- Use an answer-first lede when the digest gives one strong fact and the reader needs it fast.
- Stay inside the provided material; a narrow, fact-bounded update can still be complete.
- Preserve the clean payoff for the reader instead of widening into unsupported analysis.
- Match the insider voice by stating the confirmed fact directly, without source-meta language.
- Keep the story centered on the immediate team consequence when it is directly supported.
- For insider pieces, a clear injury or roster-interest update can carry the article if the next-step question is immediate and grounded.
- Strong grounding can offset middling execution when the core development is clear and relevant.
- Clean execution matters most when the source material contains enough verifiable substance to support the angle.
- When the source gives only one solid checkpoint, a concise “looks good / returned to practice” update can still work if it stays tightly factual.

## What to avoid
- Do not add source-narration phrasing like “according to sources” or “the report did not add much beyond the sighting itself” when the article can state the fact directly.
- Do not let headline, intro, and opening paragraph drift into separate versions of the same update.
- Do not import extra names, dates, or performance lines that are not supported by the digest.
- Do not turn one injury or interest note into broader team-direction or roster-planning analysis unless the source supports it.
- Do not frame a reported fact with unsupported interpretive labels or implications.
- Do not bury the main update behind setup or meta commentary.
- Do not repeat the lede instead of advancing the most useful consequence or next step.
- [insider] Do not reach for explanatory context outside the digest; keep the piece inside the confirmed material.
- [insider] Avoid adding secondary-defense or ripple-effect framing when the source only confirms a single interest/injury fact.
- Do not stack a headline, lede, and body sentence on the same vague idea; each should add something new.
- If the digest is missing the needed facts, trim the story rather than filling gaps with inferred offseason or expiring-deal detail.
- If the source digest is effectively empty, do not try to salvage the piece with a thin paraphrase loop; cut it instead. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
