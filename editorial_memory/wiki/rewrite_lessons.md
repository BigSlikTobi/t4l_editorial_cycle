# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open with the single clearest verified development first, especially when the source only supports one narrow process or status update.
- Lead with the concrete decision, absence, or timeline detail instead of vague framing about broader fallout.
- Keep the copy tightly source-bound when the digest is thin; brevity works if every line is factual.
- Let the confirmed development do the work rather than adding explanatory filler or extra interpretation.
- Use the exact verified status or process detail to anchor the lede.
- Keep headline and lede aligned to the same confirmed event, while letting the lede add the cleanest source-backed detail.
- Preserve a direct, factual tone when the source only supports a limited update.
- A concise process or injury lead can work well when the source clearly establishes the subject and the basic timeline or absence.
- When the source is specific, clean breaking-news phrasing can carry the item without extra context or scene-setting.
- Strong grounding matters most: if the headline and intro stay inside the verified facts, a short update can still read well.

## What to avoid
- Do not pad a thin update with vague alignment, fallout, or roster language; stay on the concrete verified note.
- Do not widen a single confirmed process update into broader meaning, finality, or team posture unless the digest directly supports it.
- Do not add extra interpretation about what the team is “presenting” or “signaling” when the source only gives a narrow process detail.
- Do not bring in unrelated broader context just to make a short report feel fuller.
- Do not let headline and intro drift into repetitive restatement without adding a new source-backed detail.
- Do not bury the main verified point under setup or meta language.
- Do not treat a thin digest as support for a larger narrative arc.
- Do not over-explain the implications when the source only supports a single takeaway.
- Avoid unsupported embellishment around injuries or timelines; keep the absence/window exactly to what the source says.
- [analyst] Do not let serviceable but plain phrasing become repetitive; keep the headline and intro sharp enough to match the persona.
- [analyst] Do not lean on meta-style phrasing about what the report does or does not say when the digest has no extra substance.
- [analyst] If the source only offers a broad framing, treat it as source-substance-limited and do not try to manufacture a stronger hook from prose alone.
