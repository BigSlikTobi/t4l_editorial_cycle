# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the headline, subhead, and lede aligned on one verified development; a clean package makes a small update publishable.
- Lead with the most concrete angle already present in the source instead of a generic summary of a “strong impression” or “early mark.”
- A single concrete hook can carry the piece when the fact pattern is specific and well grounded.
- Stay inside the supplied fact pattern from start to finish; tight grounding is a recurring strength.
- Direct, fact-first writing works best when the source gives one clear point rather than a broad trend.
- Preserve a clear line from headline to body so the reader immediately sees the core update.
- When the source is narrow, let one specific detail do the work instead of trying to expand the frame.
- Strong specificity can offset modest impact if the execution stays disciplined.
- A clear, source-only angle can work when the headline, intro, and body all point to the same verified context.
- Clean insider execution succeeds when it stays specific, grounded, and free of unsupported claims.
- [insider] Anchor quickly to the confirmed development and support the lede with the body instead of wandering beyond what the source supports.
- [analyst] A narrow contract update can still read well if it stays bounded to the verified unresolved premise and avoids adding structure the digest never gave.
- [columnist] A columnist piece can work when it uses a concrete on-team angle and stays away from broad impression-language.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a small update into broader speculation or theme-driven interpretation that the source does not support.
- Don’t pad a thin fact with generic “why it matters” language if the digest does not supply it.
- Don’t repeat the headline verbatim in the subhead or intro; avoid redundant phrasing.
- Don’t let filler language create facts, momentum, or extra certainty the source never supplied.
- Don’t overstate consequences or imply follow-on developments unless the digest explicitly supports them.
- Don’t bury the strongest factual takeaway under broad summary language or meta commentary.
- Don’t force a broader frame when the real story is one confirmed development.
- Don’t add unsupported process or next-step framing when the source only confirms the immediate decision.
- Don’t treat thin source material as a license to infer more than it says; keep the claim boundary tight.
- [insider] Don’t build beyond an empty or sparse digest, even if the headline looks specific; missing support makes the story ungrounded (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t introduce source-narration or Yahoo/report framing that reads like uncertainty padding; cut meta wording and state only the source-supported claim.
- [insider] Don’t write a lawsuit/police-conduct story as if the digests confirm the allegations when the source set has no usable fact content; that is ungrounded from the start (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t lean on generic framing when the source is effectively empty; if the digest offers only one supported fact, keep every sentence anchored to it.
- Don’t introduce roster-conversation speculation or broader profile claims that go beyond the supported first-impression fact.
- [columnist] Don’t use column-style sweep to imply larger conclusions from a sparse digest; keep the argument bounded to the one verified early impression.
- [columnist] Don’t run the headline, subhead, and intro with the same broad “early mark/strong impression” claim; make one of them carry the concrete detail and cut the repetition.
- [analyst] Don’t invent role labels, negotiation structure, or other added scaffolding around a bare contract premise; if the digest only confirms unresolved deals, write exactly that.
- [analyst] Don’t imply extra certainty by turning a simple unresolved-contract note into a detailed breakdown of separate talks unless the source explicitly supplies it.
- [analyst] Don’t name unsupported players or entities to fill out a contract story; if the closed-world inputs don’t confirm them, the piece is ungrounded (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t lose credit for a strong source-only update by adding unsupported details; the winning move is to stay precise, not to expand.
