# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the clearest verified development first; a grounded football fact can carry a thin update.
- Lead with concrete stakes, actions, or status changes rather than editorial framing.
- Keep headline and lede aligned to the same confirmed event, but make sure the lede adds a cleaner angle or new detail.
- Use the most specific source detail available to anchor a short update.
- Stay tight when the story only supports one development; brevity is a strength if it is focused.
- Preserve a factual, source-bound tone when no extra context is available.
- Let the confirmed status change or transaction do the work rather than inflating it with commentary.
- [insider] When the digests support a real team-specific stake plus a clear status update, a concise grounded lead can still read well even without extra flourish.
- [insider] Keep the piece centered on the verified absence/status, the relevant team context, and any explicit contract-talk update when those are all supported.
- [analyst] When multiple verified facts point to a real on-field or roster stake, it can support a stronger-angle lead if the copy stays grounded.
- [analyst] Concrete numbers and trade-offs can support a tougher-path angle when the source digests clearly establish them.
- [columnist] A sharp, specific wrinkle can work as the story’s center when the rest of the copy stays tightly source-bound.

## What to avoid
- Do not repeat the headline almost verbatim in the lede; add new information or a cleaner angle.
- Do not pad a thin update with vague filler or generic “more on the story” language.
- Do not bury the only confirmed development under setup or meta language.
- Do not widen a single confirmed note into speculation about broader team plans, roster implications, or company strategy.
- Do not add unsupported context just to make the story feel fuller.
- Do not use source-meta phrasing like “according to the report” when the copy can simply state the verified fact.
- Do not force analysis onto a report that only supports a factual transaction, quote, status item, injury note, or name change.
- [insider] Do not state a reported link or reunion as a completed move; hedge it as a report unless the source explicitly confirms the addition.
- [insider] Keep the headline and intro inside the verified facts when the source only supports a tie or link; avoid “added” language.
- [insider] Do not present a rumored reunion as established when the digest has no supporting detail; that is source-side, not fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] Do not stack unresolved injuries, protection concerns, or roster questions into a vague “tougher path” frame unless the facts are clearly established and the wording stays clean.
- [columnist] Do not restate the same core fact in both the intro and body without adding a distinct, source-backed angle.
- Do not lean on banned source-narration or meta language when there are no verifiable facts to support the piece.
- Do not invent a larger era-shift, event significance, or player context when the source digest is effectively empty; that is source-side, not fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
