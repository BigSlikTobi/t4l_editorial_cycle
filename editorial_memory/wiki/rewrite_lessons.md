# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the verified roster move immediately; put the designation up front.
- Keep headline and lede aligned on the same confirmed core claim.
- Make the headline and lede work as separate jobs rather than repeating the exact same sentence.
- Name only the players explicitly listed in the source when the story is roster-specific.
- Stay tight on the roster designation itself when the source is sparse.
- Use clean, direct wording for simple transaction updates.
- Keep the opening centered on the team action instead of drifting into package material.
- Preserve precision in insider/transaction copy; small verified details matter more than broad framing.
- When the source is limited, a narrow, factual lede is stronger than an inflated angle.
- Frame the update as an immediate availability note when that is the real news peg.

## What to avoid
- Do not add Yahoo/report/source-meta phrasing or other report-style narration.
- Do not layer in unsupported roster implications or context beyond the designation itself.
- Do not use filler like a “paperwork” or “next item to watch” aside unless the source clearly supports it.
- Do not let the headline, sub-headline, and intro all repeat the same basic fact pattern.
- Do not let an empty or thin digest drive speculative detail.
- Do not broaden a roster transaction into a larger story without source support.
- Do not mix verified names with unverified surrounding claims.
- Do not pad a sparse update with invented context or analysis.
- Do not treat a limited-source item as if it confirms more than the roster move.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the source material is effectively empty, the fix may require source cleanup rather than rewriting around it.
