# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the concrete roster or status change when the source is clear; a new arrival, clearance, or availability shift can carry the piece.
- Keep the lede tightly centered on the most source-backed fact instead of widening into extra camp or league context.
- Surface the key implication in the first sentence or two so readers understand the update immediately.
- When the source gives a clear timing or camp-development note, use it to sharpen the news value without overexplaining.
- Keep team-specific updates direct and answer-oriented when they give readers a real roster or availability answer. [insider]
- Stay strictly within what the digests support when the story is source-thin; a clean fact-bounded item is better than a forced expansion. [insider]
- Preserve uncertainty when the source does not support extra timing, outcome, or contextual claims.
- Strong grounding can offset moderate scores elsewhere if the article stays anchored to the reported facts.
- [analyst] Put the most consequential number- or decision-led angle in the headline and intro, then use secondary watch points as support.
- [insider] If the digest supports a clear status or roster-development update, it is enough to build a publishable item around without padding.
- [insider] Support the core claim with named players and roster context when the digests clearly validate it.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into broad camp commentary or generic league context when the source offers a sharper status implication.
- Do not let the headline or intro stay too serviceable; if there is a clearer availability or roster consequence, make that the spine.
- Do not bury the most distinctive source-backed status change behind continuity or background paragraphs.
- Do not add unsupported team-history or career-path details just to flesh out the item.
- Do not mix in extra context to make a short update feel bigger than it is.
- Do not repeat the same short update across headline, subhead, and intro; compress cleanly and move only useful extras deeper in the piece.
- Do not turn a source-light story into a columnist-style take; if the digest does not support the stakes, the framing is not salvageable in prose.
- Do not leave unsupported player-history or team-history claims in place; remove them rather than trying to salvage the angle.
- [analyst] Do not miss the clearest number-led or decision-led angle when the source digests provide it.
- [insider] Do not use a team transaction to smuggle in unsupported roster or depth-chart conclusions.
- [insider] If the provided source digests cannot validate the core reported fact, the issue is source-side, not a prose fix.
- [insider] If the digest is effectively empty, strip the piece back to only what is directly supported or do not publish; do not invent a full-clearance or camp-ready timeline from the headline alone.
