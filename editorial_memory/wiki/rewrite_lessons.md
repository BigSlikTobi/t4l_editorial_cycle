# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest camp stakes or status change when the source gives one; a health update, roster battle, or role shift can carry the piece.
- Keep the lede tightly centered on the most distinctive source-backed facts instead of stretching into generic camp context.
- When the source contains usable numbers or role implications, surface them early for an analyst-style payoff. [analyst]
- A sharp, specific headline/lede combo improves the piece more than broad “watch list” framing.
- Build around concrete source-backed facts rather than broadening into significance or narrative.
- Preserve uncertainty when the source does not support extra timing, outcome, or contextual claims.
- Strong grounding can offset moderate scores elsewhere if the article stays anchored to the reported facts.
- Keep team-specific updates direct and answer-oriented when they give readers a real roster or contract answer. [insider]
- [insider] Include only confirmed deal or status details that materially change the update; do not pad with unrelated context.
- [analyst] Put the main analytic angle in the headline and intro, then use secondary watch points as support.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into broad camp commentary or generic league context when the source offers sharper roster stakes.
- Do not let the headline or intro stay too serviceable; if there is a clearer decision point, make that the spine.
- Do not bury the most distinctive source-backed number, competition, or role implication behind a continuity paragraph.
- Do not add unsupported timing, outcome, timetable, or decision certainty beyond the confirmed facts.
- Do not mix in extra context just to make a short update feel bigger than it is.
- Do not repeat the same short update across headline, subhead, and intro; compress cleanly and move only useful extras deeper in the piece.
- Do not lean on process language or generic framing when the digests support a more specific analytical angle.
- Do not turn a source-light story into a columnist-style take; if the digest does not support the stakes, the framing is not salvageable in prose.
- [analyst] Do not miss the clearest number-led or decision-led angle when the source digests provide it.
- Do not leave an unsupported identity or status claim in place; correct it or remove it rather than trying to salvage the angle.
- [insider] Do not use a team transaction to smuggle in unsupported roster or depth-chart conclusions.
- [insider] Do not name specific surrounding players or scenarios unless those details appear in the source digests; otherwise treat them as source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [insider] If the provided source digests cannot validate the core reported fact, the issue is source-side, not a prose fix.
