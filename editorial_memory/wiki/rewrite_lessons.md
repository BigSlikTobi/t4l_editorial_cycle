# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the headline and lede aligned on one verified fact so the piece feels controlled from the first line.
- Build around the exact confirmed point when that is the strongest available source fact; let that single fact carry the piece.
- A concise opening that stays faithful to the source is often stronger than trying to manufacture added context.
- Lead with the clearest supported takeaway rather than widening into background the source does not fully establish.
- Trim speculation and keep only lines directly anchored in the reported remark or item.
- Even a single-source item can support a readable piece if the framing stays narrow and precise.
- [insider] A clean rewrite can save a draft when it strips out unsupported status claims and rebuilds from only source-digest facts.
- [insider] When the report is usable but thin, keep the lede tightly centered on the report itself and avoid inflating it with extra certainty.
- [analyst] When the digest is thin, a restrained evaluation can work if it stays inside the single grounded fact.
- [analyst] Preserve the two confirmed poles of the update when source facts are limited; a simple “relief plus concern” frame can be enough if both are explicitly supported.
- [analyst] Keep injury-note coverage on the verified signal only when the digest is sparse; narrow framing is stronger than forced context.

## What to avoid
- Do not let the headline or lede promise inevitability, certainty, or a broader trend when the source only supports one statement.
- Do not stretch a quote or item into unsupported extrapolation about direction, ownership, or reader reaction unless the source explicitly supplies it.
- Do not widen a narrow source fact into a full-report treatment; if the material is thin, keep the article equally thin and focused.
- Avoid speculative connective tissue that is not directly tied to the confirmed statement.
- Do not add background color just to make the story feel bigger if that context is not actually sourced.
- Keep the story from implying a more developed report than the evidence provides; clarity matters more than breadth.
- If the source is only a single remark or item, do not force multiple consequential takeaways from it.
- [insider] Do not assert a date, status certainty, or roster implication when the source digest does not support those facts.
- [insider] Cut back follow-on lines that turn a bare contact report into a confirmed-team-need or transaction story.
- [insider] If the digest is incomplete, cut the headline and intro back rather than filling gaps with assumed facts (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not build an article around named-player/team/status claims when the source digest is effectively empty or only repeats the headline summary (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not introduce a player into the story context as if they belong to the team/article when the digest gives no factual basis for that connection (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] When the source digest contains no usable facts beyond the headline-level claim, treat the item as non-publishable rather than trying to rescue it in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] Do not use loaded evaluation labels unless the digest actually anchors that level of certainty.
- [analyst] Avoid implying confirmed consequences from a thin report; keep the language to the grounded fact and the questions it legitimately raises.
- [analyst] Don’t widen a simple note into broader concern or a team-specific angle when the digest cannot support it.
- [analyst] Avoid extra roster/quarterback framing when the source only confirms the headline-level injury signal; keep the piece on the two verified facts only.
- [analyst] Don’t use “the only clear takeaway” or similar certainty language when the source does not fully establish that conclusion.
- When the source digest is effectively empty, treat the item as non-publishable rather than trying to rescue it in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] If the digest is empty or near-empty, do not infer broader injury meaning, timeline, or transaction implications from a headline-level mention alone (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] Don’t repeat headline-style report language when the digest adds no verified substance; if grounding is zero, there is no recoverable core for a rewrite (source-side, not fixable in prose).
