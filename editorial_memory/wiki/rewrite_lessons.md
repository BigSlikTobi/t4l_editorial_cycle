# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the one confirmed development first, especially when the source is a digest or otherwise thin.
- Keep headline and lede aligned on the same supported fact, while making sure the lede adds something the headline does not.
- Use a direct, restrained tone when the source only supports one clean takeaway.
- Stay inside the source’s exact scope; narrow inputs can still make strong stories if the framing is disciplined.
- Let the confirmed quote, audio, or action carry the hook instead of layering on extra explanation.
- Keep the lede concise and avoid front-loading interpretation before the fact is stated.
- Strong sparse-source pieces stay at quote-level or fact-level confirmation and do not force extra color.
- Concrete status updates can work when every claim is explicitly grounded.
- [insider] Wire-style execution works when it is precise, concrete, and fully inside the digest.
- [columnist] A restrained columnist voice can still work when it stays tightly grounded and lets the confirmed detail do the work.
- When the source is only report-deep, hedge transaction language to match what is actually confirmed.

## What to avoid
- Don’t let the headline and lede repeat the same claim without advancing the story with a new source-backed detail.
- Don’t add unsupported consequences, broader implications, or inferred fallout when the source confirms only one development.
- Don’t let the lede drift into summary language that goes beyond the digest.
- Don’t pad a thin source with industry-wide or team-wide implications unless the source explicitly supplies them.
- Don’t overbuild context around a source-light story; keep the framing tightly source-bound.
- Don’t infer motive, hidden details, or off-page specifics from a limited digest.
- Don’t use meta narration about what the source item does or does not spell out.
- Don’t let unsupported phrasing weaken grounding; precision matters more than flourish in sparse-source stories.
- Don’t bury the one confirmed takeaway under scene-setting, extrapolation, or speculative significance.
- Don’t repeat the same claim across headline, intro, and body without adding new source-backed detail; thin stories still need a distinct step forward.
- [insider] Don’t waste space on generic setup or self-referential filler; get straight to the concrete update.
- Don’t add source-narration about what the digest included or omitted; strip meta lines and keep any follow-up sentence as a plain continuation of confirmed facts.
- Don’t convert a source gap into certainty in the body or headline; missing support is a source-side problem, not a prose flourish issue. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- Don’t state a transaction as fully completed if the source only supports a reported connection; match the hedge to the evidence.
- Don’t force team context into a digest that doesn’t support it; that gap is source-side, not fixable in prose. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- [analyst] Don’t build a system-change story when the digest is effectively empty; no anchor means no publishable angle.
- Don’t assert an outcome like a release when the digest only supports the arrest and named parties; unsupported disposition claims are a grounding failure.
- Don’t use source-meta phrasing such as “according to the digest” or “No additional details were included”; keep the prose in-story and plain.
- Don’t let a rewrite keep the right names but add an unsupported procedural step; the fix is to narrow the claim, not decorate it.
- Don’t describe a bare digest as “tied to” a person or transaction unless the source actually substantiates that link; circular hedge language is still unsupported framing.
- Don’t add report-level context when the only facts are headline-deep; if the rewrite still can’t add source-backed detail, the piece is a dismissal, not a styling problem.
- Don’t contradict the source headline with a different outcome or status; if the digest says released, the draft cannot treat the same event as only an arrest.
- Don’t turn a clean sign-only update into a sourced-feeling mini feature by layering in extra team-state context that the digest doesn’t confirm.
- Don’t repeat a signing or transaction claim on a closed-world digest when the source provides no facts at all; that is a grounding failure, not a wording issue.
- Don’t treat a cleanly framed piece as salvageable if the only digest is empty; no amount of polish can support a claim with zero source facts. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- [insider] Don’t publish from an empty source digest: repeating the headline allegation or naming a subject without verifiable facts leaves grounding at zero and is not fixable by rewrite.
