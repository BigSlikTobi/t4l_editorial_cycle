# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the one verified development and make the subject/result relationship unmistakable in the first sentence.
- Keep headline and lede tightly aligned around the same confirmed angle.
- A plain status/injury statement can carry the piece when the source package only supports one clear update.
- Tight, source-grounded openings work even when the digest is thin.
- Use minimal presentation when the story has only one usable takeaway.
- Strong grounding matters more than style flourishes when the report is narrow.
- Stay anchored to the confirmed event instead of widening into broader roster, lineup, or team-story context.
- A wire-style lede can succeed when it states the outcome plainly without embellishment.
- Correct entity framing and immediate event setup can carry a piece when there is only one confirmed fact.
- [insider] Center the lead on the single verified injury/status outcome rather than adding recovery or lineup speculation.
- [insider] Keep the article short and direct when the package is thin; a tightly grounded note can still clear if it stays specific.

## What to avoid
- Do not publish as though multiple concrete details are confirmed when the source digest is effectively unusable or empty.
- Do not treat an unsupported digest or mismatched source title/URL as proof of injury specifics.
- Do not assert recovery timelines, lineup impact, or other specifics that the source package does not explicitly support.
- Do not misstate the team or entity field; a wrong label can sink an otherwise usable piece.
- Do not add roster names or roster context that the source package does not support.
- Do not pad a narrow update with extra framing about rhythm, timing, or broader team narrative.
- Do not let the lede circle the source event instead of getting to the verified takeaway.
- Do not widen a straightforward item into a bigger team story without explicit source support.
- Do not bury the verified development under unsupported details that read like confirmed facts.
- Do not overbuild a simple story with surrounding context that the package never establishes.
- [insider] If the source is thin, keep the article short and direct; unsupported specifics make the piece harder to save on rewrite.
- [insider] Cut meta phrasing about added detail or broader roster decline unless the package directly supports it.
- [insider] When the package supports only one fact, rebuild around that fact rather than layering in extra names or context.
- [analyst] Avoid fantasy-impact framing when the source provides no facts to support the claim.
- [analyst] Do not frame systemic consequences when the source package contains no factual basis for them (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not force a quarterback-rotation or alternate-starts story unless a source_digest actually supports it.
- [insider] If the digest has no usable facts, rebuild the headline and intro from verified material or skip the story entirely.
- [insider] When the digest is effectively empty, treat it as a source-substance failure, not a prose problem (source-side, not fixable in prose).
