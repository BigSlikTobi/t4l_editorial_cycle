# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the real decision point or payoff, not a generic setup, and let that question drive the lede.
- Stay tightly inside the source digests; grounded framing scores well when the facts are clean.
- Use supporting examples to illuminate the main debate, not to pad the piece.
- Keep the story centered on the practical football question the source actually raises.
- Let a strong verified development supply the hook instead of forcing a bigger angle.
- Concrete, source-backed specifics can carry the article when they directly support the main update.
- Preserve high-specificity coverage when the reporting is strong and the fact pattern is coherent.
- Keep headline and intro aligned on the same verified development without duplicating it in near-identical language.
- [analyst] Build around the concrete roster/playing-time decision itself; the clearest hook can be the decision threshold, not a broader narrative.

## What to avoid
- Do not start with broad summary language when the source points to a sharper decision or outcome.
- Do not restate the same core claim in the headline, subhead, and intro; one clean lede should do the work.
- Do not add background examples unless they genuinely clarify the central point.
- Do not pad the package with extra restatement before reaching the actual news.
- Do not overextend the lead with role labels or framing that the source does not clearly support.
- Do not present roster, status, or readiness claims as firmer than the digest allows.
- Do not sharpen the headline so much that it simply mirrors the intro.
- Do not rely on unsupported metadata framing or team-assignment claims; those are source-side, not fixable in prose.
- Do not try to build a story from an empty digest or headline-only note alone; if the source has no usable facts, the piece is not writable.
- [analyst] Do not let the analysis wander away from the practical decision point; keep the tension anchored to what must happen next.
