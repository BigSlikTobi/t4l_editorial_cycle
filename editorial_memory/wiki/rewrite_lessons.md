# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the verified camp injury or role/workload decision immediately when the source supports a clear, concrete takeaway.
- Keep headline and lede aligned on the same confirmed fact, but make each one do separate work.
- For insider/analyst pieces, sharpen the team-specific angle instead of stopping at a generic status recap.
- Use the headline or intro to surface the real conflict, evaluation window, or decision when that is actually sourced.
- Trim repetitive phrasing so the lede advances beyond the headline instead of echoing it.
- Keep the story grounded in the source-digest claim while tightening the editorial angle around it.
- Use restrained, direct wording for camp-management and quarterback-evaluation items.
- When the source has enough substance, turn it into a concrete takeaway rather than a bland summary.
- Preserve clarity over flourish, but avoid flattening the piece into a near-paraphrase of the source.
- When the source is well-supported, make the implication explicit without overexplaining.

## What to avoid
- Do not restate the same sentence in multiple forms; the lede must add new value.
- Do not leave an insider/analyst story at a generic source-digest level when there is a sharper team-specific takeaway available.
- Do not underuse the headline or intro; they should highlight the main conflict, injury, evaluation, or decision, not repeat a bland summary.
- Do not make the piece feel mechanically grounded but editorially thin.
- Do not hide the real angle by focusing only on the broad status update.
- Do not let repetitive phrasing dilute the actual news.
- Do not keep the copy so cautious that it misses the verified injury or competition angle.
- Do not flatten a meaningful camp update into a generic recap.
- Do not overbuild context beyond the source, but also do not settle for a bare restatement.
- Do not imply an investigation, injury cause, or other unsupported detail when the source digest does not actually provide it (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not write as if the only job is to summarize; the insider persona should surface the actionable takeaway from the source facts.
- [insider] Do not rely on unsupported inferential language to bridge gaps in the source.
- Do not use source-meta phrasing like “Yahoo report” or “the report ties” when the source itself does not supply publishable facts.
- Do not invent factual details from an empty digest; if there is no source-grounded event, the piece is not salvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
