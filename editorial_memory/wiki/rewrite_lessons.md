# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the story anchored to one confirmed development when the source package is thin.
- Lead with the most concrete, source-backed update first; don’t bury the live development under setup.
- Use direct breaking-update framing when the sourced facts support only one main angle.
- Align headline, lede, and body on the same verified point.
- Keep the article restrained and factual when the source support is limited.
- Preserve precise wording around the central fact instead of stretching it into broader implications.
- A tight hook can come from a coach/source saying a team pursued a player for a long stretch, especially if the player is now practicing or in the building.
- [insider] Lead with the confirmed football development and immediate team activity, not speculation about the bigger move.
- [insider] A long-running pursuit can be a strong hook when it is explicitly sourced and tied to a current on-field update.
- [insider] Keep the lede inside the verified material even for a blockbuster name; simple and source-faithful can still work.

## What to avoid
- Don’t add move details, terms, or roster implications unless the source digest clearly supports them.
- Don’t let a thin source package drive broad framing choices; narrow the story instead.
- Don’t include unsupported meta language about the sourcing or reporting process.
- Don’t mention unrelated names or side references unless they are directly tied to the confirmed facts.
- Don’t make the headline promise a bigger development than the body can verify.
- Don’t pad a simple update with extra context that isn’t in the source material.
- Don’t turn a verified update into analysis or implication without source support.
- Don’t repeat the headline idea in the lede; move to the newest confirmed detail.
- Don’t rely on source-meta filler to bridge missing detail; if the source is sparse, write only the confirmed development and stop there.
- [insider] Don’t imply trade mechanics or deal terms from a summary that only confirms interest and current practice.
- [insider] Don’t use unsupported player-name references as connective tissue; if a name isn’t in the sourced update, leave it out.
