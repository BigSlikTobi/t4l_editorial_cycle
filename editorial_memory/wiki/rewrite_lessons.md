# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the single confirmed update immediately when the source gives one clean, usable fact.
- Keep the lede tightly anchored to the named source’s confirmation, especially for recovery/timeline items.
- Make headline and lede align on the same verified fact, while each adds a little new value.
- Use the headline/intro to sharpen the real event or stakes instead of a vague status recap.
- Keep insider pieces pointed at the team-specific takeaway, not a generic digest of the news.
- Preserve a restrained, direct tone on camp-management, injury, and quarterback-evaluation items.
- When evidence is solid, turn a sourced claim into a concrete takeaway rather than leaving it flat.
- Stay inside the confirmed fact set when the source is thin; precision beats reach.
- Make the implication explicit only when it is clearly supported by the source.
- [insider] Give the immediate team-specific implication plainly, without meta language or unnecessary inference.
- [analyst] Let a record-level or contract-level fact carry the angle when the source supports it, and keep the piece tightly grounded.
- [analyst] Make contract-year or evaluation stakes do real work only when the source gives concrete support.

## What to avoid
- Do not restate the same confirmed point in multiple forms; the lede must add new value.
- Do not introduce phrases that imply more source certainty than the digest actually provides.
- Do not lean on meta wording like “the report ties” or similar framing unless the source itself supports it.
- Do not add broad team outlook language when the source only confirms a narrow update.
- Do not start with a vague setup when the story already has a sharper confirmed hook.
- Do not let the piece stay at a generic source-digest level when a clearer team-specific takeaway is supported.
- Do not underuse the headline or intro; they should spotlight the main confirmed development, not repeat a bland summary.
- Do not make the copy feel mechanically grounded but editorially thin.
- Do not pad a thin digest with inference, roster-planning claims, or negotiation meaning beyond the source.
- [analyst] Do not turn a record-level offer into roster-construction or priority claims unless the digest directly supports them.
- [insider] Do not write as if the only job is to summarize; surface the actionable takeaway from the source facts.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Do not try to manufacture a story from a source that only offers a bare denial or missing factual support.
- [analyst] Do not present first-shot or first-opportunity notes as settled roles or certainties when the source digest lacks concrete support.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Do not build an article on an unsupported or internally broken premise when the digest is missing the key confirming facts.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Do not force named-person/team specifics into the draft if the provided source facts do not actually establish them.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Do not fill gaps with unsupported player mentions, camp/offseason filler, or extra specifics when the digest only confirms the headline-level deal.
