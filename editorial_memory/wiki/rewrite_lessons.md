# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on one verified news hook and keep the lede centered on that single development.
- Use a narrow, source-backed angle; a specific injury, start, signing, suspension, or status update is stronger than a broadened take.
- Keep headline and lede tightly aligned so the same confirmed fact carries both.
- Thread in a few concrete, source-supported details to add density without widening the story.
- Preserve a direct, factual tone when the source supports a clean news brief.
- Keep the body focused on the announcement and the verified immediate context, not on broader roster interpretation.
- A strong direct quote can carry the piece when it is the most concrete source-backed detail.
- Lean on the source’s clearest standard or rationale instead of paraphrasing around it.
- [analyst] A quarterback-start or competition update works when it stays on the named decision and verified resume facts.
- [analyst] Drafts read well when they balance specificity with restraint: enough detail to show why the update matters, not enough to overreach.
- [insider] Injury, suspension, discipline, or practice-ejection coverage works when it stays anchored to the confirmed event and named people, with only source-backed immediate context.
- [insider] Tighten the prose around the confirmed action first; direct, factual phrasing is stronger than meta framing about the source.
- [insider] Keep on-the-record quotes intact when they directly support the team-specific takeaway.
- [insider] A clear, team-specific read can emerge from a tightly grounded source without drifting into unsupported context.

## What to avoid
- Do not add unsupported player, contract, roster, or reaction details just to make the update feel fuller.
- Do not treat a headline-level summary as if it confirms extra context or side claims.
- Do not widen a simple announcement into broader competition, fallout, or roster-meaning language unless the digest supports it.
- Do not let the headline and lede drift beyond the specific verified event.
- Do not stack extra contextual claims that are not explicitly in the source set.
- Do not imply sequencing or implications more strongly than the digest supports.
- Do not introduce side names or examples unless the source digest directly supports them.
- Do not overstate what the announcement says about job security, future implications, or organizational response.
- Do not rely on banned-source narration, meta language, or source-context filler; keep the rewrite in article voice.
- Do not repeat the same takeaway in multiple sentences; tighten the middle and move on.
- [insider] Cut phrases like 'the report says' or 'named in the report'; they add source narration without adding substance.
- [insider] If the source digest is missing key support, treat extra player-specific framing as a grounding failure, not as a writing flourish.
- [insider] Do not publish contract-extension numbers or terms unless the source digest explicitly supports them (source-side, not fixable in prose).
