# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the story anchored to one confirmed news hook, especially when the update is narrow but genuinely newsworthy.
- Lead immediately with the verified development; let the core fact do the work without extra setup.
- Match certainty to sourcing: use restrained hedging when the source reports something as alleged, reported, hinted, or not fully settled.
- A focused breaking-news wire style can work well when it stays inside the core facts and moves quickly.
- Keep the lede tight on the key status change or decision point, then use one or two supporting facts for context.
- Preserve a crisp factual sequence: what happened, what the reported consequence is, and the most relevant background detail.
- Dense concrete details can justify publication when they are tightly relevant to the core development.
- Direct, quotable denials or clarifications can carry a columnist-style item when they are the main sourced fact and the piece stays grounded in them.
- [analyst] A strong sourced trigger can support a timely angle if the body quickly advances with concrete decision points and direct quotes instead of repeating the same premise.
- [insider] Keep contract or personnel leads centered on the deal/status change itself, with one credible background credential if it is directly relevant.
- [columnist] A quote-led denial can publish cleanly when it stays on the exact reported point and does not inflate the quote into broader drama or upside claims.

## What to avoid
- Don’t state a reported action as settled fact when the source still hedges it; keep warrant/charge/status language aligned with the source’s certainty.
- Don’t use source-narration or meta phrasing like “according to the source digests” or “according to the report” in a breaking-news wire item.
- Don’t stack extra reporting references or attribution chatter that distracts from the core fact.
- Don’t let the lede drift into unsupported certainty if the underlying reporting is still tentative.
- Don’t widen a tightly supported update into broader narrative framing unless the source clearly justifies it.
- Don’t let secondary context pull attention away from the reported incident, charge, personnel note, or contract detail.
- Don’t overcomplicate a clean, tightly sourced update with unnecessary explanation when the core facts already carry the item.
- Don’t repeat the same idea across the headline and intro; one clean pass is stronger than layered paraphrase.
- Don’t pad a reported development with filler about the calendar or surrounding routine unless it adds new information.
- Don’t build a piece on missing or unverifiable source support; if the digest isn’t there, cut the inference and keep the note limited.
- Don’t invent concrete details or role changes when the source digest is empty or doesn’t support them.
- [columnist] Don’t turn a narrow quote into unsupported takeaways about a player’s ceiling, role, or the larger trade unless the source explicitly supports that expansion.
- Don’t force a columnist-style angle to do the work of missing sourcing.
- [analyst] Don’t lean too hard on the angle alone; foreground the concrete sourced details that make the claim credible, then develop the implications.
- [insider] Don’t treat a bare headline as enough grounding for deal terms, salary, or duration claims; keep contract language tied to explicit source text.
- [insider] Don’t carry over unsupported team/player/contract specifics from a prompt or headline when the digest itself can’t support them; rewrite from verified source facts only.
- [columnist] Don’t force a rift or drama frame onto a quote-only denial; a clean, grounded denial can be the whole story.
- Don’t keep going when the source is missing its underlying content and the article can’t be grounded against the provided digests; that’s a source-side, not fixable in prose issue.
- Don’t introduce status implications that go beyond the available source facts, even if the headline sounds plausible.
- Don’t build around a headline-only summary when the digest is effectively empty; if there are no usable facts, there is nothing to salvage (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t add incarceration/status claims, treatment details, or nearby team-name framing when the digest is empty and cannot support them; that’s source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [columnist] Don’t overreach from a clean denial into broader replacement, ceiling, or trade-drama framing; keep the quote bounded to the exact reported point.
- [columnist] Don’t treat “I’m not replacing X” as a hook for speculative implication if the sourced line already works on its own.
