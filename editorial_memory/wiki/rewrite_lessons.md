# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the one confirmed development immediately; make the first sentence do the main reporting work.
- Keep the story tightly grounded in the source’s core fact, especially for straightforward update items.
- Use a report-first, wire-style frame when the source supports one clean disputed fact.
- Keep the lede tightly focused on the exact verified claim instead of broadening into surrounding angles.
- Let one clear development remain the center of the piece, with only the context needed to support it.
- Keep headline and intro aligned on the same core claim without repeating them verbatim.
- Strong grounding can carry a simple breaking-news item; clarity beats embellishment.
- Specificity helps when it stays inside the verified facts; precise wording is a strength only if the source actually supports it.
- For reveal-style or quote-led stories, a clean source-first angle can work if the lede stays on the exact quoted claim.
- [insider] A clean breaking-news structure works when it stays tightly inside the verified status update and the core fact is already source-supported.
- [analyst] When the source supports only a minimal take, keep the article tightly centered on the one supported development rather than forcing broader roster implications.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into extra context, implication, or consequence unless the source clearly supports it.
- Do not turn a clean status update into source-meta narration or commentary about what the material did not include.
- Do not let confidence outrun the digest; hedge contract terms or specifics when they are not actually confirmed.
- Do not add resolution, timing, or follow-through unless the source explicitly supplies it.
- Do not repeat the headline’s claim in the lede without adding new information.
- Do not let the headline, subhead, and body all say the same thing; avoid near-duplicate tap lines.
- Do not pad a simple item with generic filler when the verified development already supplies the news value.
- Do not invent extra detail just to make the story feel fuller.
- Do not add unsupported named-entity references or connective devices just to create color or momentum.
- Do not lean on vague or meta-style lines when the source only supports one concrete disputed claim.
- [insider] Do not include planning implications or source-narration/meta lines like “no broader football angle” or “what the source material did not include”; keep to the confirmed update only.
- [insider] Do not salvage a story by talking around the source; if the report is supported, stay direct and report the dispute cleanly.
- [insider] Do not build an article from a digest that has no usable facts in closed-world input; that is a source-side, not fixable in prose, problem.
- [insider] Do not introduce unsupported team/player metadata, especially when it is not present in the digest.
- [insider] Do not repeat the same claim across headline, subhead, and intro without adding sourced substance.
- [analyst] Do not let roster-competition pieces drift into generic restatement; if the source only supports a minimal take, avoid extra roster spillover language that is not explicitly backed.
- [analyst] Do not stack unsupported names or side topics into the lede/body just to make the story feel broader; cut them unless the source explicitly supports them.
- [analyst] Do not present a competition as resolved when the provided material does not actually support that conclusion.
- [analyst] Do not frame a battle around specific names when the digest is empty or otherwise lacks source-supported facts.
- [columnist] Do not write status-style evaluations or breakout claims about named players when the digest only supports a preview/watchlist framing.
- [columnist] Do not invent roles, spots, or camp takeaways from a thin digest; if the source lacks key facts, keep the piece as a modest preview rather than a facts-heavy assessment.
- [columnist] When the source is sparse, keep the headline and intro framed as a watchlist, not as proof of who is winning camp jobs.
- [columnist] Avoid generic camp-language filler that implies knowledge the source does not provide.
