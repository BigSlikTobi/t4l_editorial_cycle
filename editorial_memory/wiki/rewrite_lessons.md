# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the story tightly inside the source’s exact quote or confirmed fact, especially on columnist pieces.
- Lead with the concrete news peg the source actually supplies, then stop there unless the digest adds more.
- Make the headline and intro share the same factual hook without drifting into broader narrative.
- Use a direct, specific headline when the source gives a clean quote or clear event.
- Preserve a crisp factual sequence: exact statement first, minimal context second.
- Let one well-supported line carry the piece instead of expanding into bigger transition or “next chapter” framing.
- Strong named specifics can help, but only when every detail is directly supported by the source package.
- A tightly grounded quote-driven update can still read well when it avoids overexplaining the implications.
- [columnist] Keep the piece anchored to the speaker’s exact wording and the immediate, stated takeaway.
- [analyst] When the source offers only one real decision point, make that point explicit and keep every sentence serving it.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a tightly supported update into a larger transition or roster-change story unless the source clearly supports it.
- Don’t add “new chapter,” “next version,” or similar framing when the digest only gives a smaller, immediate quote.
- Don’t infer a broader strategic meaning from one coach quote unless the source actually spells it out.
- Don’t stack extra narrative around a clean source line; use the quote, not a storyline built beyond it.
- Don’t let the headline promise more context than the intro can verify.
- Don’t imply roster implications, role shifts, or future direction that the digest does not directly state.
- Don’t overexplain a simple sourced remark with strategic language that isn’t in the source package.
- Don’t turn a source-supported comment into a “moving on” or “what comes next” article without evidence.
- [columnist] Don’t import broader significance or personal-arc framing when the source only supports a narrow quote-driven angle.
- [columnist] Don’t treat named-player mentions as safe support unless they are directly grounded in the provided facts; otherwise soften or cut them.
- (source-side, not fixable in prose) Don’t try to manufacture a bigger story when the digest only supplies a brief quote and no supporting follow-through.
