# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with tightly grounded updates and stay inside the digest’s confirmed facts.
- A clean two-update package can work when each item is concrete and clearly sourced.
- Keep the headline and intro aligned on the same verified takeaway; one sharp angle is enough.
- Preserve hedges and uncertainty when the source does not confirm a final decision.
- Include the key status detail in the opening, then add the second confirmed update without overexplaining it.
- Strong grounding can carry a piece even when the item is modest in scope.
- Keep the prose free of source-meta language; let the reported facts do the work. [insider]
- Add only the confirmed status details that matter, especially in camp/injury-style updates. [insider]
- Clear, factual first sentences work well when the source supports more than one related development. [insider]

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow camp update into broader narrative or significance claims without support.
- Do not let the headline promise more certainty than the source provides.
- Do not drop source hedges when the digest only supports a conditional or unresolved status.
- Do not add unsupported timing, return, or decision certainty beyond the confirmed facts. [insider]
- Do not lean on process language, structural explanations, or behind-the-scenes commentary unless the digest explicitly supports it.
- Do not mix in extra context just to make a short update feel bigger than it is.
- Do not repeat the same update across headline and intro; compress cleanly and move only useful extras deeper in the piece. [insider]
- Do not treat source-grounded camp notes as a place for meta commentary about the article itself.
- Do not infer roster or role certainty beyond what the digest explicitly confirms. [insider]
- Do not make team-specific claims unless the source clearly supports them; keep the scope tied to the reported update. [insider]
- Do not fabricate a centerpiece or build around a subject absent from the provided source digests; that is a source-side failure, not fixable in prose.
- Do not stack unsupported injury-status specifics, recovery timelines, or matchup-based return windows on top of an empty factual base.
