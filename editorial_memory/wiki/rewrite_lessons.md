# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the single clearest verified development first; a grounded fact can carry a thin update.
- Keep the headline and lede aligned to the same confirmed event, but let them do different jobs.
- Lead with the concrete action, quote, or outcome rather than editorial framing.
- Use the most specific source detail available to anchor a short update.
- Stay tight when the story only supports one development; brevity is a strength if it is focused.
- Preserve a factual, source-bound tone when no extra context is available.
- Let the confirmed status change or transaction do the work rather than inflating it with commentary.
- Keep insider copy clean and direct when the only support is a confirmed move, contract, availability update, injury note, or name change.
- [insider] A season-ending injury/surgery update works when it stays on the confirmed medical fact and the resulting roster move, with no extra stretch-run framing.
- [insider] Missed-practice reports can work when they state the concrete availability fact plainly and stop there if no further injury detail is provided.
- [columnist] A sharp quote can be the story’s center if the rest of the framing stays grounded in that exact line.
- [columnist] In roundup-style pieces, a league-wide or source-scope frame can work if it matches the digest and the headline/intro avoid inventing a team-specific angle.

## What to avoid
- Do not repeat the headline almost verbatim in the lede; add new information or a cleaner angle.
- Do not pad a thin update with vague filler or generic “more on the story” language.
- Do not bury the only confirmed development under setup or meta language.
- Do not widen a single confirmed note into speculation about broader team plans, roster implications, or company strategy.
- Do not add unsupported context just to make the story feel fuller.
- Do not use source-report language that weakens the directness of a clean, grounded update.
- Do not over-explain why the piece is short; keep the prose focused on the confirmed fact.
- Do not force analysis onto a report that only supports a factual transaction, quote, status item, injury note, or name change.
- [insider] Do not state an injury, surgery, or season-ending status as confirmed unless the digest explicitly supports it; keep the headline and intro inside the verified facts.
- [insider] When the source only confirms missed practices or another limited availability fact, do not infer severity, timeline, or “questions persist” framing.
- [columnist] Do not misidentify the subject’s role or attach an unsupported team/function label; fix the grounding first before any stylistic cleanup.
- [insider] Avoid layering in implications, team-wide meaning, or public-facing-strategy language; keep the exact confirmed deal/item as the center.
- [insider] Avoid vague editorial scaffolding like “according to the report” when the source already gives the concrete update.
- [insider] If no further details are reported, say that plainly instead of dressing it up.
- [insider] Fix any team-reference mismatch or internally inconsistent source labeling before publication; never carry a roundup item with the wrong team attached.
- [columnist] Don’t impose a team-specific angle on a league-wide roundup when the digest only supports the broader scope.
- [analyst] Don’t add matchup framing, implications, or takeaways when the source summary is effectively empty; unsupported analysis can’t be rescued from a bare digest (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t build a full narrative from a headline-level source digest alone; if the source has no usable facts, the story is not salvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t rely on unsupported team-tag, arrest, or bounce-back framing when the source digest is empty or misaligned; that is source-side, not fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t publish specific injury-timeline or player/team-context claims when the digest is missing; that is source-side, not fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t genericize a roundup around named people when the digest has no verifiable development; an empty source deck is not enough to make a publishable story (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [columnist] Don’t frame reconciliation, reunion, or anniversary significance when the digest is empty or marked missing; that’s source-side, not fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t lean on banned source-narration or meta language when there are no verifiable facts to support the piece.
- Don’t replace missing facts with broad emotional or historical generalities; if the digest is empty, the draft cannot be rescued in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [columnist] Don’t add unsupported team-specific context or season framing when the digest is effectively empty; if the source can’t support it, the piece is not salvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [columnist] Don’t introduce unrelated named entities or connections when the digest is effectively empty; unsupported associations can’t be repaired in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t leave a dismiss-worthy item at placeholder level when the source headline supplies a clear verified development; if the digest supports the central fact, the lede must state it directly.
- [insider] Don’t say the digest has no further details and stop there when the report already confirms the core development; underwritten copy that omits the headline fact is unusable.
