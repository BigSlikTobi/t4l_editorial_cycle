# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the verified core event immediately, with the main update clear in sentence one.
- Keep the lede tightly anchored to what the source explicitly confirms, especially for timely personnel or ceremony updates.
- Preserve uncertainty when the source is tentative; do not upgrade conditional language into certainty.
- Lead with the practical next-step implication when the source actually supports one. [insider]
- Keep the story tied to the specific game, role, practice setting, or event the source addresses.
- Use a clean, direct frame for straightforward updates rather than inflating them into broader narratives.
- Align headline and lede on the same verified angle without simply repeating identical wording.
- Keep body copy grounded in the digest and advance with new source-backed detail instead of restating the lead.
- Let a stable, well-sourced item read as a usable update, not a notebook dump.
- When grounding is strong, it can support a clear, team-specific football angle without straying beyond the source facts.
- For analyst pieces, concrete supporting details in the body can strengthen the story without overreaching.
- When a story is quote-driven, build around the exact quoted line and let that do the work.

## What to avoid
- Do not add unsupported medical, roster, camp, background, or named-player context that the digest does not confirm.
- Do not broaden a narrow update into a larger narrative about implications or next steps unless the source supports it.
- Do not let speculative phrases like broad "what happens next" language outrun the actual facts.
- Do not restate the same core fact in the lede and follow-up paragraphs without adding fresh source-grounded detail.
- Do not bury the main event under extra context when the source already gives a clear angle.
- Do not treat fallback reps or contingency plans as interchangeable with confirmed decisions.
- Do not overstate status when the source language is still conditional or incomplete.
- Do not make headline, subhead, and lede repeat the same update without adding new information.
- Keep the team/entity field matched to the story's real subject; a wrong-team mismatch can sink the piece even if the body is grounded.
- If the source digest is empty or internally unreliable, stop rather than infer facts; treat that as a source-side problem, not a prose fix (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Avoid banned meta phrasing like "the source material" or "the report" when the facts are already clear; write the clean news brief directly.
- When the source is thin, close on the last confirmed detail rather than introducing process language or editorial commentary.
- For quote-led items, do not add unsupported explanation about what the quote means for the team unless the source directly supports that step.
- If source support is thin, strip out precise jail/probation-style details rather than trying to infer them from context (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not pad a confirmed announcement with unsupported career praise, atmosphere, or tribute color; stick to the verified development.
- Do not let intro and first paragraph drift into different versions of the same event; keep them matched to the same plain, source-backed fact.
- Avoid pulling in unrelated named examples from elsewhere; if the source digest is empty, the fix is to remove those names and stay with the one confirmed angle (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Cut report-style filler and wire-meta phrasing that dulls the news brief voice; push the verified event and immediate context into the opening.
- Avoid DRY lead/headline/subhead setups that repeat the same exact frame instead of advancing the angle.
- When a timely personnel update also has a clear team-need angle, surface both in the opening rather than leaving one to the body.
- Do not keep unrelated player mentions or implied details when the available source only confirms the core injury/recovery timeline.
- Do not overbuild ceremony/recognition items into broader analysis when the source already gives a concrete event, timing, and team context.
- Do not frame a story around absence, contract tension, or similar significance when the source digest is effectively empty; that is source-side, not fixable in prose.
- Do not infer roster or contract meaning from no evidence; if the digest is empty, dismiss rather than speculate about why the update matters.
- Do not underplay a concrete roster-development angle when the sourcing clearly supports it; make the team need or practical consequence visible early.
- For [insider], pair the verified event with the immediate roster/organizational context when both are sourced, rather than burying the significance.
- Do not force a rewrite into generic caution if the digest supports a specific, tightly grounded update; keep the angle crisp and concrete.
- Do not leave a cleanly newsworthy injury timeline unsupported; if the digest is missing the confirming facts, dismiss rather than embellish the claim (source-side, not fixable in prose).
