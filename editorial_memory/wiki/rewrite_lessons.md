# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the core verified development immediately; make the first sentence carry the main reporting load.
- Keep the story tightly grounded in supplied digests, especially when the update is straightforward and fan-relevant.
- Use concrete supporting details to substantiate the update, rather than broad generalities.
- Let one clear development remain the center of the piece, with only the context needed to support it.
- Include specific background facts when they directly explain why the update matters.
- Keep headline, subhead, and intro aligned on the same core claim.
- Finished-report framing works when the digest already provides the essential news value.
- Strong grounding can carry a piece even when the angle is simple; clarity beats embellishment.
- [insider] Build around the confirmed status update and use supporting details to deepen it, not replace it.
- [insider] Specific backstory plus a clear current status can make a camp update feel complete without overreaching.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into extra context, implication, or consequence unless the source clearly supports it.
- Do not let confidence outrun the digest; match certainty to what is actually confirmed.
- Do not add resolution, timing, or follow-through unless the source explicitly supplies it.
- Do not turn a clean status update into broad commentary or source-meta narration.
- Do not repeat the headline’s claim in the lede without adding new information.
- Do not let the headline, subhead, and body promise more than the source material provides.
- Do not pad a simple item with generic filler when the verified development already supplies the news value.
- Do not invent extra detail just to make the story feel fuller.
- [insider] Do not claim more confirmation than the digest supports; stay inside the verified status update.
- [insider] Do not carry the story beyond the supported camp/update framing into unsupported roster or outcome implications.
