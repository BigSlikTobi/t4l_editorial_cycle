# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the headline and lede aligned on one verified development; a clean, source-tight package is often enough to publish.
- Lead with the most concrete source-supported development instead of meta framing or broad impression language.
- Stay inside the supplied fact pattern from start to finish; tight grounding is a recurring strength.
- A single concrete hook can carry the piece when the fact pattern is specific and well grounded.
- Direct, fact-first writing works best when the source gives one clear point rather than a broad trend.
- Preserve a clear line from headline to body so the reader immediately sees the core update.
- When the source is narrow, let one specific detail do the work instead of trying to expand the frame.
- A clear, source-only angle can work when the headline, intro, and body all point to the same verified context.
- [analyst] Center the piece on the clearest ranking or evaluation reason the source gives, and keep any supporting context tightly attached to that same verified hook.
- [insider] Anchor quickly to the confirmed development and support the lede with the body instead of wandering beyond what the source supports.
- [columnist] Lead with the verified news cleanly, even on a sharp or sensitive item; a grounded first sentence beats source-narration.
- [columnist] A specific, relevant quote can be enough when it cleanly establishes the core split or update.
- [insider] A direct wire-style lede with immediate roster or contract consequences can carry the piece when the digest is fully grounded.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a small update into broader speculation or theme-driven interpretation that the source does not support.
- Don’t pad a thin fact with generic “why it matters” language if the digest does not supply it.
- Don’t repeat the headline verbatim in the subhead or intro; avoid redundant phrasing.
- Don’t let filler language create facts, momentum, or extra certainty the source never supplied.
- Don’t overstate consequences or imply follow-on developments unless the digest explicitly supports them.
- Don’t bury the strongest factual takeaway under broad summary language or meta commentary.
- Don’t force a broader frame when the real story is one confirmed development.
- Don’t add unsupported process or next-step framing when the source only confirms the immediate decision.
- Don’t treat thin source material as a license to infer more than it says; keep the claim boundary tight.
- Don’t make the piece sound source-backed when the digest only gives a headline-level premise; if the source is missing detail, stay tightly on the one confirmed fact.
- Don’t proceed when the source digest and story metadata point to different subjects; that mismatch is a source problem, not a prose problem (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Don’t assume a team field is usable if it isn’t supported in source material; leave it out rather than filling it in.
- [analyst] Don’t invent role labels, negotiation structure, or other added scaffolding around a bare contract premise; if the digest only confirms unresolved deals, write exactly that.
- [analyst] Don’t present an emerging-role claim or reported view as if it were verified when the source digest is missing or incomplete; unsupported reporting language is not salvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t lead with source-narration or meta lines about the digest; open on the confirmed development itself.
- [insider] Don’t add unsupported body language when the source is essentially just the summary; if the digest is thin, stay tightly on the one confirmed fact.
- [insider] Don’t present a ruling or status update as if it includes broader verified change when the digest only supports the immediate decision; keep the claim boundary narrow.
- [insider] Don’t treat an empty digest as salvageable with style or angle alone; if there are zero source facts to verify, the problem is source-side, not fixable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Don’t publish when the story has no usable source facts and the metadata points to a different subject; that’s a source-side failure, not a prose fix (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [columnist] Don’t lean on meta lines about working from the headline/summary or “the core of it”; present the verified takeaway directly.
- [columnist] Don’t use column-style sweep to imply larger conclusions from a sparse digest; keep the argument bounded to the one verified takeaway.
- [columnist] Don’t add honorific, legacy, or judgmental framing beyond the verified outcome when the source only supports the concrete update.
- [columnist] Don’t treat a precise omission/inclusion update as a big-implications story unless the source actually supplies those implications; keep the note crisp and bounded.
- [columnist] Don’t pad a headline-only premise with roster outlook or context when the digest gives no usable facts beyond the headline; unsupported detail and repeated paraphrase leave the piece ungrounded (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [columnist] Don’t publish quote-led claims, personality disputes, or relationship claims when the digest is missing; if the source is absent, the story is unverified and unsalvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
