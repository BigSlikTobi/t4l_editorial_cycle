# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the confirmed transaction or roster move, then add only the source-backed context needed to make it feel complete.
- When the source supports it, widen the angle to include the full cross-team development rather than stopping at one half of the story.
- Keep the headline and lede aligned on the same scope; if the source contains two linked moves, both should appear early.
- Use concise, source-anchored phrasing for thin insider items so the piece stays grounded and readable.
- Strong insider updates can carry multiple concrete developments if each is directly sourced and the package stays coherent.
- Keep the opening focused on the real news value instead of repeating one narrow angle in slightly different words.
- Strip unfinished, broken, or reflective filler; clean openings improve thin items fast.
- A firm role or transaction ruling is strongest when the lede states it plainly and the rest of the piece stays on that track.
- Tight grounding can still feel complete when it includes the key move plus the reported context without speculation.
- [insider] Clean, specific updates work when every detail is source-backed and the structure stays tightly on one transaction, injury, or team update.
- [insider] Paired-team breaking notes work when the package stays specific enough to capture both moves without overreaching.

## What to avoid
- Do not stop at one side of a two-team or multi-development story when the source digest clearly supplies more.
- Do not let the headline and intro cover only half the supplied story; the package should reflect the full source value.
- Do not repeat the same narrow angle in the lede after the headline has already stated it.
- Do not over-focus on a single transaction when the source also gives another relevant roster development.
- Do not invent a factual base when the source digest is empty or effectively empty; unsupported claims are not salvageable in prose.
- Do not let a narrow fact get expanded into a bigger role, motive, timeline, or consequence without concrete source support.
- Do not infer what a clearance, status note, or return means unless the source digest explicitly says it.
- Do not mismatch the headline and lede by implying more than the source actually says.
- Do not pad a small item with reflective or speculative language just to make it feel larger.
- [insider] Do not add status framing that goes beyond the digest, even if the underlying update is real.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the source digest is missing or only offers a small core fact, do not invent extra context or connective tissue to thicken the story.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Empty or effectively empty source digests are not publishable; no amount of headline polish or attribution can create a factual base.
- Do not include meta narration like “No other details were included in the source digest”; that is a banned execution pattern and should be cut, not narrated.
