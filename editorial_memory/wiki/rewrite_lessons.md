# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest verified development and keep the opening anchored to one concrete status change.
- Keep the angle tightly grounded in the digest; a simple result or practice-status update can carry the piece without added narrative.
- Match the headline to the exact sourced fact the lede is built on.
- Keep the lead short and centered on one supported takeaway instead of layering in extra interpretation.
- Preserve simple, direct status updates when the underlying fact is clear and confirmed.
- Use attribution sparingly and only as attribution; let the verified fact do the work.
- Keep the story readable by staying inside the confirmed update rather than stacking multiple implications.
- A clear team-specific reason to read can work when headline and lede stay aligned with the confirmed status change.
- Tight grounding and clean execution can carry a piece even when the angle is straightforward.
- [analyst] Tie fallout or role language to an explicit, sourced status shift, not to broader competition framing.
- [analyst] A modest Dallas-specific note can work if it stays limited to the exact fact pattern the digest supports.
- [columnist] If only one concrete update is verified, build around that update and avoid narrative embellishment.

## What to avoid
- Do not pad a thin source package with unsupported quote-style wording or faux color.
- Avoid expanding a single practice-status update into a broader battle or phase-change story without direct support.
- Do not let headline language outrun the verified facts in the body.
- Avoid adding extra texture, scene-setting, or “what it means” framing unless the digest clearly supports it.
- Do not treat source attribution as permission to import new facts from the outlet’s phrasing.
- Avoid abstract team-story framing when the source only supports a narrow roster or practice update.
- Do not build in implied significance from a thin digest; keep the claim at the level the evidence supports.
- Source gaps are not a writing problem: if the digest lacks support for a detail, leave it out rather than paraphrasing confidence into evidence.
- Avoid meta explanation and repeated restatement of the same sourced fact across headline, sub-headline, and intro.
- Avoid fabricated competition framing around a single cleared-for-work update; keep the piece at the verified status-change level.
- Do not add evaluation conclusions unless the digest explicitly states them.
- [insider] If the digest is effectively empty, do not invent camp-level or player-specific claims; that is a source-side issue, not fixable in prose.
- [insider] Do not import unsupported rivalry or quarterback-battle framing, even if the headline suggests it.
- [columnist] Do not state player-impact specifics when the digest has no factual base for them; that is a source-side, not fixable in prose.
