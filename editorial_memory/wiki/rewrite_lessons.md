# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the concrete update immediately, especially for insider items with a clear transaction, injury, hold-in, or status change.
- When the source supports it, bring the full source-backed context into the opening instead of leaving it implied.
- Keep the headline and lede aligned on the same scope; if the update has a specific cause or detail, the opening should reflect it.
- Use concise, source-anchored phrasing so thin insider notes stay grounded and readable.
- Strip filler and meta narration from the opening; clean starts make small updates feel sharper.
- A firm injury/status/transaction ruling works best when the lede states it plainly and the body stays on that track.
- Strong insider packages can carry multiple concrete developments if each is directly sourced and the structure stays coherent.
- Tight grounding can still feel complete when it includes the key move plus the reported context without speculation.
- [insider] Clean, specific updates work when every detail is source-backed and the package stays tightly on one transaction, injury, hold-in, or team update.
- [columnist] When the digest is just a tracking item, keep the opening factual and name the listed subjects plainly instead of trying to add reporting weight.
- [columnist] A short sourced quote can carry a small news item when the lede stays inside the exact wording and the headline matches that narrow fact.
- [columnist] Quote-led notes work when the lede foregrounds the exact sourced line and keeps the framing narrow.

## What to avoid
- Do not stop at one side of a two-item or multi-development story when the source digest clearly supplies more.
- Do not let the headline and intro cover only half the supplied story; the package should reflect the full source value.
- Do not repeat the same narrow angle in the lede after the headline has already stated it.
- Do not lean on generic attribution like "reports Tuesday" when the source-backed framing is already available.
- Do not add meta narration about the report or roundup; cut it rather than narrating the process.
- Do not overstate what a tracking item provides when the digest has no usable facts beyond the headline.
- Do not let the opening sound like a wrapper around the headline instead of a grounded news item.
- Do not invent a factual base when the source digest is empty or effectively empty; unsupported claims are not salvageable in prose.
- Do not let a narrow fact get expanded into a bigger role, motive, timeline, or consequence without concrete source support.
- Do not infer what a clearance, status note, or return means unless the source digest explicitly says it.
- Do not mismatch the headline and lede by implying more than the source actually says.
- Do not pad a small item with reflective or speculative language just to make it feel larger.
- [insider] Do not add roster-inference language or extra player-name framing unless it is directly supported by source facts.
- [insider] Do not let the headline, intro, and body drift outside the closed-world facts; keep all three strictly inside what the digests confirm.
- [columnist] Do not use source-meta framing or implied added reporting when the digest is only a tracker.
- [columnist] Don’t write as if a live-update headline confirms details the digest doesn’t actually provide.
- [columnist] Do not turn a single sourced quote into an implication of decision or permanence; stay inside the exact wording.
- [columnist] If the source gives one core fact, do not repeat it mechanically across headline, subhead, and lede; make each layer earn its keep.
- [columnist] [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the source digest is missing key facts or only offers a headline-level premise, do not invent scene-setting, connective tissue, or specifics like a named habit.
- [columnist] [source-side, not fixable in prose] A title/content mismatch or similarly shaky grounding means the source record itself is unreliable; prose fixes cannot make it solid.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Empty or effectively empty source digests are not publishable; no amount of headline polish or attribution can create a factual base.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] A readable multi-item roundup still fails if the allowed record cannot verify the facts behind it.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Dismissed items built on an empty digest cannot be rescued by rewriting; the problem is the missing source record, not execution.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Don’t treat a headline-level tracker as if it contained verified names or a defined set of details when the digest itself is empty.
