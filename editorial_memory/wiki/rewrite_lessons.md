# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the story anchored to one confirmed news hook, especially when the update is narrow but genuinely newsworthy.
- Match certainty to sourcing: use restrained hedging when the source reports something as alleged, reported, hinted, or not fully settled.
- Lead with the specific verified development and let that carry the item without extra framing.
- A focused breaking-news wire style can work well when it stays inside the core facts and moves quickly.
- Keep the lede tight on the key status change or decision point, then use one or two supporting facts for context.
- Strong grounding can carry a story even when impact is modest, as long as the piece stays clean and direct.
- Clear, audience-facing wording works best when it avoids speculative expansion.
- Preserve a crisp factual sequence: what happened, what the reported consequence is, and the most relevant background detail.
- Reported legal, disciplinary, or personnel updates work when the prose stays precise about what is confirmed versus what is only reported.
- Dense concrete details can justify publication when they are tightly relevant to the core development.
- [analyst] A strong sourced trigger can support a timely angle if the body quickly advances with concrete decision points and direct quotes instead of repeating the same premise.
- [analyst] Stay tightly inside the sourced discussion when the angle is the news; multiple direct, source-backed quotes can make a modest-impact item feel publishable.

## What to avoid
- Don’t state a reported action as settled fact when the digest still hedges it; keep warrant/charge/status language aligned with the source’s certainty.
- Don’t use source-narration or meta phrasing like “according to the source digests” in a breaking-news wire item.
- Don’t stack extra reporting references or attribution chatter that distracts from the core fact.
- Don’t let the lede drift into unsupported certainty if the underlying reporting is still tentative.
- Don’t build an inference-heavy lede when the source digest has no accessible facts to support the claim.
- Don’t widen a tightly supported update into broader narrative framing unless the source clearly justifies it.
- Don’t let secondary context pull attention away from the reported incident, charge, or personnel note.
- Avoid implying a verified status change beyond what the reporting actually supports; keep the prose tightly hedged when the source is thin.
- Don’t overcomplicate a clean, tightly sourced update with unnecessary explanation when the core facts already carry the item.
- Don’t build a piece on missing or unverifiable source support; if the digest isn’t there, cut the inference and keep the note limited.
- Don’t invent concrete details or role changes when the source digest is empty or doesn’t support them.
- Don’t force a columnist-style angle to do the work of missing sourcing.
- [analyst] Don’t lean too hard on the angle alone; foreground the concrete sourced details that make the claim credible, then develop the implications.
- [insider] Don’t fabricate injuries, season-ending status, or depth-chart consequences when the source digest contains no confirmable facts at all (source-side, not fixable in prose).
