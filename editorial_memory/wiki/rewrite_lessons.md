# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest source-backed update when it has a crisp hook; a single confirmed fact can carry the item.
- Keep the headline and lede tightly aligned so the reader gets the main point immediately.
- Use the strongest source-supported detail early, especially when it adds a specific moment, date, or setting.
- When the source gives a clean answer, a compact fact-bounded item is stronger than padded framing.
- Preserve the clean payoff for the reader; stay on the story the source actually supports.
- Strong grounding can offset moderate scores elsewhere if the article remains anchored to the reported facts.
- [analyst] Put the most consequential number- or decision-led angle in the headline and intro, then use secondary watch points as support.
- [analyst] A tightly grounded, specific team update can clear even if the broader package is modest, as long as the key reported stake is clear.
- [insider] If the digest supports a clear status or roster-development update, it is enough to build a publishable item around without padding.
- [insider] Support the core claim with named players and roster context when the digests clearly validate it.
- [insider] Keep team-specific updates direct and answer-oriented when they give readers a real availability answer.
- [insider] Match headline, subhead and intro to the same verified timing; a clean contract/status update is stronger than a muddled chronology.
- [insider] Stay tightly inside the digest on injury/reporting items: deliver the confirmed diagnosis or status immediately and avoid adding unsupported recovery/timetable detail.
- [insider] Concrete source-backed contract or cap context can strengthen an availability update when it stays subordinate to the verified status.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into broader report framing or extra team context when the source offers a sharper confirmed fact.
- Do not let the headline or intro stay too generic; if the source provides the most compelling detail, make that the spine.
- Do not bury the strongest source-backed hook behind unrelated material or detours.
- Do not repeat the same setup across headline and intro; compress cleanly and move only useful extras deeper in the piece.
- Do not add unsupported injury aftermath, timetable, or recovery claims.
- Do not mix in off-topic or unrelated league items unless they are directly tied to the story’s reported angle.
- Do not turn a source-light story into a columnist-style take; if the digest does not support the stakes, the framing is not salvageable in prose.
- Do not leave unsupported player-history or team-history claims in place; remove them rather than trying to salvage the angle.
- [analyst] Do not miss the clearest number-led or decision-led angle when the source digests provide it.
- [insider] Do not use a team transaction to smuggle in unsupported roster or depth-chart conclusions.
- [insider] Do not invent or misattribute quotes or source-style attributions; keep only confirmed facts from the digests.
- [insider] If the provided source digests cannot validate the core reported fact, the issue is source-side, not a prose fix.
- [insider] If the digest is effectively empty, strip the piece back to only what is directly supported or do not publish; do not invent a full-clearance or camp-ready timeline from the headline alone.
- [insider] Clean up lede/date handling when the timing is muddy; avoid quote-framing that feels like source copy rather than clear reporting.
- [insider] Do not add return-timetable speculation when the source only confirms the injury or status change.
- [insider] Do not overreach from a practice-participation note into unsupported speculation; keep the update anchored to the verified level of participation and contract context.
