# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the single verified event or status update and make the takeaway obvious in the first sentence.
- Keep headline and lede tightly aligned on the same confirmed angle without repeating themselves verbatim.
- A plain, wire-style statement can carry the piece when the source package supports only one clear fact.
- Short, source-grounded openings work even when the digest is thin.
- Minimal presentation is a strength when the story has only one usable takeaway.
- Strong grounding matters more than style flourishes when the report is narrow.
- Stay anchored to the confirmed event instead of widening into broader team, roster, or camp commentary.
- Correct entity framing and immediate event setup can carry a piece when there is only one confirmed fact.
- [insider] Center the lead on the single verified outcome rather than adding speculation about what happened next.
- [analyst] When the source supports one concrete rotation or usage update, state the pecking order plainly and keep the takeaway centered on the confirmed role.

## What to avoid
- Do not publish as though multiple concrete details are confirmed when the source digest is effectively unusable or empty.
- Do not treat an unsupported digest or mismatched source title/URL as proof of specific details.
- Do not add invented narration, causal language, or extra color when the source only supports one basic fact.
- Do not misstate the team or entity field; a wrong label can sink an otherwise usable piece.
- Do not pad a narrow update with broad commentary about the team, camp, or narrative around it.
- Do not let the lede circle the source event instead of getting to the verified takeaway.
- Do not widen a straightforward item into a bigger story without explicit source support.
- Do not bury the verified development under unsupported details that read like confirmed facts.
- Do not overbuild a simple story with surrounding context that the package never establishes.
- [insider] If the source is thin, keep the article short and direct; unsupported specifics make the piece harder to save on rewrite.
- [analyst] Avoid fantasy-impact framing when the source provides no facts to support the claim.
- [analyst] Do not frame cap, roster, or other systemic consequences when the source package contains no factual basis for them (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] Do not assign a quote or approach to a named source unless the source digest explicitly supports that attribution; unsupported attribution weakens grounding.
- [analyst] Avoid generic filler about flexibility or similar soft framing when the article can be built around one concrete takeaway.
- [analyst] Do not repeat the same idea in headline, intro, and body without adding new source-grounded detail.
- Do not leave meta language about the digest, source package, or lack of facts in the published copy; rewrite around the confirmed angle instead.
- Do not use explanatory filler that reads like a prompt response when the piece should just present the verified angle.
- Do not add stats, quotes, or details that are not present in the source digests.
- [analyst] If the digest offers no factual support beyond the headline, treat any precise backfield framing as unsupported and rebuild from verified material only (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not build a story on an empty factual base or repeat claims the digest cannot substantiate; if the package is headline-only, it is not publishable as substance.
- Do not lean on meta framing about what the source "keeps the focus on" when the copy already lacks hard facts.
- Do not try to rescue a headline-only package by dressing a single unsourced claim up as if more were known; if rewrite has already failed, the gap is source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [columnist] Avoid heated-scuffle or similar punchy framing when the digest does not supply concrete supporting details; do not overstate conflict without source support.
- [columnist] Do not build a column on one unsourced confrontation claim and then amplify it with certainty or scene-setting the source never gives.
