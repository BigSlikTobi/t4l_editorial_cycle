# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest verified roster or injury development and keep the opening anchored to one concrete status change.
- Keep the angle tightly grounded in the digest; a simple injury, practice-status, or contract move can carry the piece without extra narrative.
- Match the headline to the exact sourced fact the lede is built on.
- Keep the lead short and centered on one supported takeaway instead of layering in interpretation.
- Preserve simple, direct updates when the underlying fact is clear and confirmed.
- Use attribution sparingly and only as attribution; let the verified fact do the work.
- Keep the story readable by staying inside the confirmed update rather than stacking multiple implications.
- A clear team-specific reason to read can work when headline and lede stay aligned with the confirmed development.
- Tight grounding and clean execution can carry a piece even when the angle is straightforward.
- [analyst] A roster-case or contract-cap update works best when it stays specific to the sourced development rather than generic evaluation language.
- [analyst] Keep open-competition, roster-bubble, or cap-exit updates direct and compact; the sourced fact can carry the story without speculation.
- [insider] A first-game-back return plus a positive evaluation can work when the lede stays fully inside the confirmed facts.

## What to avoid
- Do not pad a thin source package with unsupported quote-style wording, faux color, or invented certainty.
- Avoid expanding a single practice-status, roster-status, or contract-mechanics update into a broader battle or phase-change story without direct support.
- Do not let headline language outrun the verified facts in the body.
- Avoid adding extra texture, scene-setting, or “what it means” framing unless the digest clearly supports it.
- Do not treat source attribution as permission to import new facts from the outlet’s phrasing.
- Avoid abstract team-story framing when the source only supports a narrow roster, practice, or cap update.
- Do not build in implied significance from a thin digest; keep the claim at the level the evidence supports.
- Source gaps are not a writing problem: if the digest lacks support for a detail, leave it out rather than paraphrasing confidence into evidence.
- Avoid repetitive setup and restatement of the same sourced fact across headline, intro, and body.
- Avoid inference-heavy depth-chart or cap-certainty; keep the claim tied to the exact sourced point.
- Do not add evaluation conclusions unless the digest explicitly states them.
- [analyst] Do not claim a player “strengthened his case” or “helped himself” when the digest is empty; that is unsupported evaluation language built on no facts.
- [analyst] If the source digest has no usable facts, treat the story as unsalvageable rather than trying to infer an outcome or cap consequence from the headline.
- [analyst] Cut any player or team references that are irrelevant to the story; off-target names can break relevance even if the angle is otherwise workable.
- [insider] If the digest is effectively empty, do not invent camp-level or player-specific claims; that is a source-side issue, not fixable in prose.
- [insider] Do not import unsupported rivalry or quarterback-battle framing, even if the headline suggests it.
- [insider] Do not state player-impact specifics when the digest has no factual base for them; that is a source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [insider] Cut meta commentary about what the source report adds; keep the story to the confirmed return and evaluation.
- [insider] If the lede says it's the first game back, make sure that claim is explicitly supported and not an unsupported embellishment.
- [insider] If the source digest is effectively empty, do not force a roster-change writeup; treat it as a source-side, not fixable in prose issue.
- [insider] Avoid implying roster consequences unless the digest explicitly supports that consequence.
- [insider] Do not use meta sentences about the digest or source limitations inside the story; that is banned-style commentary, not reporting.
- [analyst] An empty digest is a hard stop for cap-path or release-math speculation; unsupported contract consequences should be treated as source-side, not fixable in prose.
