# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open with the verified move and make the main subject/entity relationship unmistakable in the first sentence.
- Keep headline, lede, and body aligned on the same confirmed angle.
- Tight, source-grounded openings work when the digest supports only one clear update.
- A plain advisory/status statement can carry the piece when the source is narrow.
- Stay anchored to the confirmed event instead of widening into broader roster or team narrative.
- Use minimal presentation when the source only offers one usable takeaway.
- Strong grounding matters more than style flourishes when the report is thin.
- [insider] Put the concrete team consequence in the first sentence when that is the story’s real value.
- [insider] A wire-style lead can succeed when it states the expected action plainly without embellishment.
- [insider] Correct entity framing and immediate event setup can carry a piece when there is only one confirmed fact.
- [columnist] A concise, tightly grounded note can still clear if it stays specific and avoids unsupported interpretation.

## What to avoid
- Do not publish when the source digest is effectively empty; no verified material means the piece cannot be grounded.
- Do not generate a story from missing or blank source material; there is no factual substrate to rewrite from (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not assert contract figures, team moves, or transaction context that the provided digest does not support.
- Do not misstate the team or entity field; a wrong team label can sink an otherwise usable piece.
- Do not add source-meta filler like “no further details were provided” into the story.
- Do not add roster names or roster context that the source digest does not support.
- Do not pad a narrow update with extra generic framing about rhythm, preseason, or timing.
- Do not repeat the same basic fact in multiple ways when one clean statement would do.
- Do not let the lede circle the source event instead of getting to the verified takeaway.
- Do not widen a straightforward item into a bigger team story without explicit source support.
- Do not bury the verified development under unsupported details that read like confirmed facts.
- Do not overbuild a simple story with surrounding context that the digest never establishes.
- [insider] When the source supports only one fact, rebuild around that fact rather than layering in extra names or context.
- [insider] If the digest is thin, keep the article short and direct; unsupported specifics make the piece harder to save on rewrite.
- [analyst] Do not frame systemic rule-change consequences when the source digest contains no factual basis for them (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [analyst] Avoid fantasy-impact framing when the source provides no facts to support the claim.
- Do not use banned source-narration/meta phrasing to explain the absence of evidence; just treat the piece as ungrounded.
- Do not invent claims about named people or entities when the digest supplies none; that is a source failure, not an execution fix.
- Do not add unsupported surrounding context to an otherwise empty digest; closed-world rewrite cannot rescue facts that were never there.
- [insider] If the source only gives a broad claim, avoid dressing it up with extra setup or season framing that the digest does not prove.
