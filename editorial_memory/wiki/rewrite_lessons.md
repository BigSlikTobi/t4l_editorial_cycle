# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the single confirmed takeaway when the source is narrow; a clean status, hire, firing, or roster move can carry the piece.
- Keep the lede tightly focused on the one verified fact instead of stretching for extra hierarchy, timing, or context.
- A source-light update can still publish well if the opening is crisp, factual, and clearly grounded.
- Keep the headline aligned with the exact confirmed takeaway; one clear hook is enough.
- Preserve uncertainty when the source does not support extra timing, market, or outcome claims.
- Build around concrete source-backed facts rather than broadening into significance or narrative.
- Let the reported facts do the work; avoid source-meta language in the prose. [insider]
- Add only the confirmed details that materially change the update. [insider]
- Clear, factual first sentences work well when the source supports a simple opening update. [insider]
- Strong grounding can offset moderate scores elsewhere if the article never drifts beyond the confirmed fact.
- Keep team-specific updates direct and answer-oriented when they give fans a real roster or staff answer. [insider]
- [insider] Keep a camp-status update centered on the confirmed availability statement, not on speculative competitive consequences.
- [insider] Include a tight, source-backed timeline detail when it is explicitly reported and helps anchor the status update.

## What to avoid
- Do not widen a narrow update into broader narrative or leaguewide significance claims without support.
- Do not let the headline or intro promise market impact, ranking, or other certainty the source does not provide.
- Do not add unsupported hierarchy or depth-chart specifics when the source only confirms the top-line order.
- Do not add unsupported injury history, diagnosis detail, or return-window language.
- Do not drop source hedges when the source only supports a conditional or unresolved status.
- Do not add unsupported timing, return, or decision certainty beyond the confirmed facts. [insider]
- Do not lean on process language, structural explanations, or behind-the-scenes commentary unless the source explicitly supports it.
- Do not mix in extra context just to make a short update feel bigger than it is.
- Do not repeat the same update across headline and intro; compress cleanly and move only useful extras deeper in the piece. [insider]
- Do not infer roster, role, or market certainty beyond what the source explicitly confirms. [insider]
- Do not surface unsupported specific player mentions or team framing in the lede or metadata when the source does not ground them (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not treat a sparse source as permission for unsupported superlatives or definitive framing.
- Do not present a status change as fact when the source has no factual support; that is a source-side issue, not fixable in prose.
- Do not add unsupported framing beyond the source digest when the event has no extractable facts; if grounding is absent after a rewrite, dismiss it rather than stretching.
- Do not build a columnist angle on team/context framing when the source digest is effectively empty; if the only support is a lone quote, treat extra setup as unpublishable.
- Do not keep rewriting around a quote when the fact base is still too thin to verify the surrounding story; dismiss instead of forcing a prose fix.
- Do not add unsupported fan-experience or reaction framing beyond what the quote plainly says; keep the claim inside the exact wording available.
- [insider] Do not turn being fully cleared into proof that a competition or evaluation outcome is already decided unless the source directly says so.
- [insider] Do not use a rehab/clearance update to imply depth-chart conclusions; keep the implication limited to availability.
- [insider] Do not leave repeated lede wording or source-summary phrasing in place when a tighter news angle is available.
- [insider] Do not add commentary about why the timing matters or how it shapes the first camp group unless that implication is directly sourced.
- [insider] Do not include source-narration/meta language in the body of a breaking-news roster update; keep it strictly transactional.
- Do not build a columnist-style take on a story when the source digest is effectively empty; without extractable facts, the framing is not salvageable in prose.
- Do not overstate certainty when the source material is only a bare headline or otherwise lacks anchoring facts; dismiss rather than inventing a premise.
- [insider] Do not make the lede sound repetitive or over-explained when the reported status plus one anchor detail is enough.
- [insider] Do not imply camp-evaluation consequences from a clearance update unless the source explicitly draws that line.
- Do not publish or try to rewrite around a roster-move story when the source digest is empty; that is a source-side issue, not fixable in prose.
- Do not force transactional assertions out of an empty digest or bare headline; dismiss when there are no grounded details to support the moves.
- [insider] Do not add unsupported roster-move specifics, sequencing, or explanations when the source provides no factual detail.
