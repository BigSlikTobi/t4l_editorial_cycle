# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest roster-battle stakes when the source supports them, especially in OTA and offseason coverage.
- Make the headline and intro sharpen the same real hook, then add a distinct new detail instead of repeating the setup.
- Keep the lede tied to the most consequential player competition the digests already emphasize.
- Use the specific job or role at issue as the organizing frame when it is explicitly supported.
- Stay grounded in the source while still being specific about what is actually being sorted out.
- Let the article move quickly from event-setting into the decision or competition the team is working through.
- [insider] In OTA coverage, center the verified battle or evaluation window rather than generic camp context.
- [analyst] A strong grounded piece can still work if it names the concrete roster questions the source already raises.
- [columnist] A restrained tone is fine as long as it sharpens the real stakes instead of flattening them.

## What to avoid
- Don’t let the headline, subhead, and intro all restate the same OTA setup without advancing to the actual roster question.
- Don’t leave a flat lede when the digests already point to a stronger competition or role battle.
- Don’t bury the most consequential player battle under generic offseason framing.
- Don’t widen a specific OTA note into broad roster conclusions beyond what the source supports.
- Don’t overemphasize camp context when the real value is in the job battles the source already makes clear.
- Don’t make the story feel repetitive by cycling through the same setup language in multiple places.
- Don’t treat a source-backed roster fight as background when it should be the main hook.
- Don’t infer extra implications from a narrow digest; keep the claim as tight as the evidence.
- Don’t convert a clear competition into vague “team is sorting things out” language.
- [insider] Don’t default to broad OTA framing when the source gives a sharper depth-chart or role competition.
- [analyst] Don’t pad the piece with repeated setup beats instead of the concrete player stakes the digests already provide.
