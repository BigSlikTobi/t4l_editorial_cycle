# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the confirmed core fact immediately when the news is simple and specific.
- Keep the headline, lede, and body on the same narrow fact set.
- Use a restrained, source-faithful frame when the story only supports one central update.
- Preserve only the exact team/status connection the source supports; short is strong when the update is clear.
- Clean grounding matters even in short updates: stay inside the verified fact pattern.
- Direct, sparse wording is often the best fit for a single confirmed development.
- Strong specificity can carry the piece when the article avoids overreach.
- When the source is tight, a compact read is better than an expanded one.
- Fit the voice to the persona while staying factual; an insider-style update can be concrete without adding embellishment.
- [columnist] Keep the treatment minimal and factual when the story is a straightforward death notice or roster-memory note.
- [columnist] A concise nod to the person’s team connection works if it stays grounded and doesn’t broaden into reflection.
- [columnist] When the article is fully supported by a death-and-roster fact pattern, tight alignment across headline, intro, and body is enough.

## What to avoid
- Do not add source-commentary about what a report did or did not include; stay on the confirmed news.
- Do not turn a straightforward death notice into meta-analysis about reporting or memory.
- Do not widen a narrow, verified fact pattern with general reflection just to add texture.
- Do not let the body drift away from the headline’s core claim.
- Do not stack extra narrative around a simple update when no additional facts are sourced.
- Do not overbuild the piece around one team connection if the source only supports that limited link.
- Do not replace grounded reporting with commentary on the reporting process.
- Do not assume background details beyond the confirmed fact pattern and the supported team/title connection.
- Do not add unsupported embellishment when the source is already complete and specific.
- [columnist] Avoid source-narration and unsupported meta in obit-style pieces; keep the article on the verified event and its direct, sourced connection.
- [columnist] Do not convert a minimal factual update into a reflective essay when the source offers no extra substance.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the available facts are too thin for a fuller piece, the limit is the source package, not just the writing.
