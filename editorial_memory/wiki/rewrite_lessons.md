# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest source-backed development when the item is narrow; one verified update can carry the piece.
- Keep the lede centered on the exact reported fact instead of widening into implied consequences.
- Match the headline and intro to the same level of certainty as the source digest.
- Use concise, source-anchored phrasing when the material only supports a tight statement.
- Preserve restraint when the source only confirms the core fact; a smaller claim can read stronger than an overbuilt one.
- Strong insider items can include multiple concrete developments if each is directly sourced and the package stays coherent.
- Fold extra practice or workout context into a second sentence/paragraph when it supports the main update rather than crowding the lede.
- Strong names plus timeline details improve utility when they remain inside the reported facts.
- Tight grounding can still feel complete when it includes status, context, and the reported explanation without speculation.
- Strip unfinished, broken, or reflective filler; clean openings improve thin items fast.
- [insider] Clean, specific updates work when every detail is source-backed and the structure stays tightly on one transaction, injury, or team update.
- [analyst] A firm role ruling is strongest when the lede states the decision plainly and the rest of the piece stays on that same track.

## What to avoid
- Do not invent a factual base when the source digest is empty or effectively empty; unsupported claims like an extension or Tuesday timing are not salvageable in prose.
- Do not let a narrow fact get expanded into a bigger role, motive, timeline, or consequence without concrete source support.
- Do not infer what a clearance, status note, or return means unless the source digest explicitly says it.
- Do not repeat the same source-backed point in the headline, subhead, and lede; each element should add something new.
- Do not repeat the same source-backed point in multiple sentences; each line must add something new.
- Do not leave the intro cut off or structurally incomplete.
- Do not pad a small item with reflective or speculative language just to make it feel larger.
- Do not add unsupported interpretive context when the source only confirms the core fact.
- Do not mismatch the headline and lede by implying more than the source actually says.
- Avoid cross-story contamination: keep the piece on the specific reported transaction/story and remove unrelated cluster references.
- Avoid forcing commentary into a piece that is only supported as a brief status or roster update.
- [insider] Cut meta sourcing language like "according to the report"; keep the sentence itself fully source-based.
- [insider] Do not add status framing that goes beyond the digest, even if the underlying update is real.
- [analyst] Cut meta language about what the source does not say; if a detail is absent, simply omit it.
- [insider] Do not state timelines, ramp plans, or role changes unless the source explicitly supports them.
- [insider] If the digest only supports a single practice-role fact, do not rebuild it into broader roster context or team-state framing.
- [insider] Tighten thin injury updates by pairing the core note with one relevant supporting detail, not three variants of the same note.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the source digest is missing or only offers a small core fact, do not invent extra context or connective tissue to thicken the story.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Empty or effectively empty source digests are not publishable; no amount of headline polish or attribution can create a factual base.
- [insider] If the digest only supplies a headline-level claim, keep the lede narrow and hedge any missing terms or deal details.
- [insider] Don’t stretch a reported extension into long-term plan or contract-status framing when the digest doesn’t support it.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] When the digest has no terms, figures, or contract specifics, treat those as absent rather than implied.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] A news peg without factual detail can support only a minimal, tightly hedged brief.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] If the digest is only a headline-level return/clearance note, do not build added support into the body; dismiss rather than embellish.
- [source-side, not fixable in prose] Empty digests cannot be rescued by adding matchup framing, player-status claims, or other unsupported body details; dismiss the piece instead.
