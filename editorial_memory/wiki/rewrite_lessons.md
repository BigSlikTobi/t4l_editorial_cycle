# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest verified development and make the opening about the concrete roster move or status change.
- Keep the angle team-specific and practical when the source supports it; a small but real roster implication can be enough.
- Match the headline to the same grounded fact the lede is built on.
- Keep the lead short and centered on one supported takeaway instead of layering extra interpretation.
- Use attribution sparingly and only as attribution; let the verified fact carry the item.
- When the source is thin, a shorter rewrite that strips away unsupported texture often performs better than trying to broaden the story.
- Preserve simple, direct status updates when the underlying fact is clear and confirmed.
- [analyst] Tie fallout language to an explicit, sourced roster or role shift, not to invented quotes or generalized meaning.
- [columnist] If only one concrete update is verified, build around that update and avoid narrative embellishment.

## What to avoid
- Do not pad a thin source package with unsupported quote-style wording or faux color.
- Avoid repeating or expanding on the same implication in multiple sentences; keep the lead tight.
- Do not let headline language outrun the verified facts in the body.
- Avoid adding extra texture, scene-setting, or “what it means” framing unless the digest clearly supports it.
- Do not treat source attribution as permission to import new facts from the outlet’s phrasing.
- Avoid abstract team-story framing when the source only supports a narrow roster update.
- Do not build in implied significance from a thin digest; keep the claim at the level the evidence supports.
- [analyst] Don’t convert a modest role battle update into broader strategic analysis without sourced backing.
- [columnist] Avoid leaning on generic praise or character labels when the digest does not verify them.
- Source gaps are not a writing problem: if the digest lacks support for a detail, leave it out rather than paraphrasing confidence into evidence.
- Avoid self-referential framing about what the piece can or cannot do; if the digest has no usable substance, the draft should not invent a story around that absence. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- Avoid asserting an ungrounded status change or leaderboard shift in a battle story; if the digest has no usable facts, do not turn the headline or lede into a claim of movement.
