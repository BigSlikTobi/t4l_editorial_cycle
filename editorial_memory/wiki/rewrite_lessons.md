# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the concrete confirmed development when it is the real news.
- Keep headline, subhead, lede, and first body paragraph aligned on the same verified update.
- Use an answer-first lede when one fact carries the whole story.
- Stay tightly inside the source material; a narrow update can still feel complete.
- Strip out meta commentary and let the confirmed fact do the work.
- Keep the piece focused on the immediate team concern when that is the only grounded consequence.
- Preserve the clean payoff instead of widening into unsupported analysis.
- Concise insider updates can work even when the source material is thin, as long as they stay factual and direct.
- Strong grounding can offset modest execution when the central development is clear and relevant.
- When the source provides only one usable checkpoint, keep the story streamlined rather than padded.
- [insider] Concrete farewell, departure, or status-change language can be enough on its own when the source gives a clear closing development.

## What to avoid
- Do not add source-narration or commentary like “the report did not add much” when the article can state the fact directly.
- Do not let headline, subhead, and lede repeat the same wording without advancing the update.
- Do not pad thin source material with generic framing that creates a false sense of added detail.
- Do not import extra names, dates, or performance lines that are not in the digest.
- Do not turn a single injury, interest, or contract note into broader analysis unless the source supports it.
- Do not bury the main update behind setup or meta commentary.
- Do not repeat the lede instead of moving to the most useful consequence or next step.
- [insider] Avoid adding explanatory context or ripple effects outside the confirmed material.
- [insider] Avoid asserting status details or concern levels that are not fully supported by the provided source material.
- [insider] Avoid unsupported player mentions or other metadata facts that the digest does not actually confirm.
- If the source digest is effectively empty, do not salvage it with paraphrase loops; cut it instead. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- If the needed facts are missing, trim the story rather than infer offseason or contract detail.
- Do not stack multiple paragraphs on the same vague idea; each beat should add something new.
- Do not invent supporting context or player mentions when the digest is empty; that is a source-side failure, not a prose fix. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
