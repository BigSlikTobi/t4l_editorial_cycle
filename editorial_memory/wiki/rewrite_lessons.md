# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the confirmed development when the item is a simple contract, extension, comeback, or depth-chart update.
- Keep headline and intro aligned on the same verified premise, but make the intro add something new.
- Use an answer-first lede when the source supports one clear takeaway.
- Tight, factual reporting can carry a sparse insider item if it stays specific.
- Ground the piece in the immediate roster or transaction consequence instead of widening into unsupported explanation.
- Let the confirmed move or status update do the work; clean execution can offset a thin source.
- Keep the update inside the source material while still giving readers the key status, timeline, or stance as stated.
- Solid name density and team-specific stakes can make a restrained piece feel complete without added speculation.
- [columnist] A clear starter/backup, extension, or status premise can be enough on its own when it is concrete and verified.
- [columnist] A crisp headline plus a non-redundant intro can make a small update feel complete.
- [columnist] When the source is thin, a restrained lede that stays inside the confirmed quote or premise is the safest approach.

## What to avoid
- Do not let the headline and intro restate the exact same sentence; the intro should advance or clarify the update.
- Do not pad thin source material with generic commentary or filler analysis.
- Do not add timelines, explanations, or extra context that the source does not support.
- Do not bury the core decision or status behind setup or meta commentary.
- Do not repeat the lede instead of moving to the most useful consequence or next step.
- Do not widen a single verified note into broader speculation about role, future, or implications unless sourced.
- Avoid unsupported claims about a player’s importance, centrality, or impact when the digest only supports the basic update.
- Avoid unsupported names, dates, or tie-ins that are not in the source.
- If the source digest is effectively empty, do not salvage it with paraphrase loops; cut it instead. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- [columnist] Avoid a body that rephrases the same premise in different words instead of adding sourced detail.
- [columnist] Avoid interpretation that goes beyond the confirmed quote or headline-level premise.
- [columnist] Avoid forcing a conclusion on an undecided status update; keep the uncertainty intact until the source resolves it.
- [columnist] Do not build a story on a missing or empty digest; if the source cannot support the named claims, the draft is ungrounded. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- [columnist] Avoid repetitive restatement when the source is thin; without verified detail, looping the premise only makes the piece feel weaker.
- [columnist] Do not treat an ungrounded rewrite as salvageable when the source itself does not verify the core premise. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
- [columnist] Do not elevate a missing-digest item into a player-significance story; if the digest is empty, claims about importance or lineup level are not supportable. (source-side, not fixable in prose)
