# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Lead with the clearest verified news peg and keep the opening focused on one concrete fact.
- Match headline and lede to the same confirmed angle so the piece feels grounded and coherent.
- Keep the lede tightly inside the source package; use only facts the digest explicitly supports.
- Let one real quote or specific reaction carry the item instead of broad narrative framing.
- Keep straightforward columnist notes short, plain, and quote-centered when the source material is thin.
- Use matchup, opponent, location, or event context only when it is explicitly part of the confirmed update.
- Build around confirmed named entities only; do not invent connective tissue to make the story feel fuller.
- Preserve sharp, simple openings for reaction or status items rather than over-embellishing them.
- [insider] Put the immediate injury or availability change first, then add only the confirmed surrounding context.
- [analyst] Keep fallout language tied to specific sourced details rather than general team-wide implications.
- [columnist] Build around a real, usable quote or reaction, but keep the framing strictly anchored to the verified facts.
- Clean, useful status updates can work when the headline stays modest and the claim is fully supported by the digest.

## What to avoid
- Do not repeat the headline in the lede or restate the same idea multiple times.
- Avoid padding thin source material with generic filler, speculation loops, or abstract commentary.
- Do not widen a narrow verified note into a bigger team story unless the source package clearly supports that shift.
- Avoid stretching a supported item into a broader tension, temperature, or atmosphere frame unless the source package clearly supports it.
- Do not assert broader response, fallout, or implications unless the digest clearly confirms them.
- Avoid blending unrelated source clusters into one narrative arc when the item only supports a tighter factual update.
- Do not let headline language outrun the confirmed facts in the lede.
- [columnist] Don’t turn a concrete human or postgame reaction into abstract team-story framing when the verified facts are already sufficient.
- [columnist] Don’t imply extra reporting detail from a thin source package; keep the piece simple and fact-only.
- [columnist] Avoid meta/source-narration tone; present the verified reaction directly without talking around the sourcing.
- Source gaps are not a writing problem: if the digest is missing key facts, don’t invent supporting detail or paraphrase confidence into evidence.
- Do not state a medical status or physical finding unless the source digest explicitly provides it; closed-world unsupported health claims should be treated as source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [columnist] When the source only supports one clear reaction quote, keep the lede and headline on that quote and cut invented scene-setting, crowd motion, or building details.
- [columnist] Avoid adding atmosphere or movement that the digest does not verify; one supported quote is better than a dramatized scene.
- [columnist] If the source package is thin, prioritize a tight fact-only rewrite over expanding the moment into a bigger narrative beat.
- [columnist] Do not build a story on speculation or lingering-name value alone when the digest provides almost no corroborated detail (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [columnist] Avoid repeating the same sentence structure across headline, intro, and lede; keep each element distinct even when they all center on the same quote.
- [columnist] Tighten around the exact verified quote and the confirmed event context; cut repetitive paraphrases and implied context that isn’t directly supported.
