# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the story anchored to one confirmed news hook, especially when the update is narrow but genuinely newsworthy.
- Match certainty to sourcing: use restrained hedging when the source reports something as alleged, reported, or not fully settled.
- Lead with the specific verified development and let that carry the item without extra framing.
- A focused breaking-news wire style can work well when it stays inside the core facts and moves quickly.
- Keep the lede tight on the key status change or charge, then use one or two supporting facts for context.
- Strong impact and specificity can compensate for a simple structure if the article stays clean and direct.
- Clear, audience-facing wording works best when it avoids speculative expansion.
- Preserve a crisp factual sequence: what happened, what the reported consequence is, and the most relevant background detail.
- Reported legal or disciplinary updates work when the prose stays precise about what is confirmed versus what is only reported.
- Dense concrete details can justify publication when they are tightly relevant to the core development.

## What to avoid
- Don’t state a reported action as settled fact when the digest still hedges it; keep warrant/charge language aligned with the source’s certainty.
- Don’t use source-narration or meta phrasing like “according to the source digests” in a breaking-news wire item.
- Don’t stack extra reporting references or attribution chatter that distracts from the core fact.
- Don’t let the lede drift into unsupported certainty if the underlying reporting is still tentative.
- Don’t bury the main development under process language about how the information was sourced.
- Don’t add layered explanation that isn’t doing real work for a short, news-first update.
- Don’t widen a tightly supported legal or incident update into broader narrative framing unless the source clearly justifies it.
- Don’t let secondary context pull attention away from the reported incident, charge, or warrant.
- Avoid implying a verified legal status beyond what the reporting actually supports; keep the prose tightly hedged when the source is.
- Don’t overcomplicate a clean, tightly sourced update with unnecessary explanation when the core facts already carry the item.
