# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open on the confirmed outcome or status change, then explain why it matters.
- Build the story around one clear verified angle when the source contains multiple possible hooks.
- Lead with the strongest factual update when it can carry the piece on its own.
- Keep the lede tightly grounded in the source digests; cleanly verified facts improve readability and trust.
- Use a vivid, source-backed gesture or detail as the hook when it directly reinforces the main update.
- Preserve high-specificity coverage when the fact pattern is coherent and well supported.
- Keep headline and intro aligned on the same verified development without repeating it in near-identical wording.
- When the source is crisp, a simple timeline or status note can be enough to make the piece useful.
- [analyst] Build around the concrete decision threshold, stat line, or finish itself; that can be the clearest hook.
- [analyst] Keep the payoff tight and source-true: a decisive result or clean statistical line can carry the article when it’s presented clearly.
- [analyst] When a game story is well supported, a concise preseason takeaway can add usefulness without overreaching.
- [insider] Keep the actionable implications limited to what the source explicitly supports.
- [insider] Tight grounding plus correct timing/eligibility details can produce a strong, wire-clean approval even without extra color.
- [insider] Mirror the report closely when the digest is thin; a precise, report-based lede can still work if it avoids overclaiming.
- [insider] A tightly sourced contract/status update can be fully serviceable when it stays specific, aligned, and free of unsupported meta-language.
- [insider] Keep the headline and intro on the agreement itself when that is the only confirmed fact; restraint can still read cleanly.
- [insider] Clean roster-move coverage works when it names only the confirmed transactions and keeps the short-term impact modest and explicit.

## What to avoid
- Do not split the lede between two unrelated story angles; pick one primary hook and commit to it.
- Do not introduce downstream implications unless the source digests clearly support them.
- Do not import outside details or adjacent storylines to create a bigger angle than the source justifies.
- Do not let the headline, subhead, and intro restate the same claim in slightly different ways.
- Do not pad the opening with extra restatement before reaching the actual news.
- Do not overextend the lead with speculation about locker-room meaning, roster movement, readiness, or follow-on consequences.
- Do not sharpen the headline so much that it merely mirrors the intro.
- Do not rely on unsupported certainty or market-value framing when the digest only mirrors a headline; soften the lede to match what is actually sourced.
- Do not mix up who plays for which team; verify player-team assignments before the final paragraph goes live.
- Do not add loose or unsupported claims that blur an otherwise strong stat-led game story.
- Do not repeat the same core game facts without enough precision in the lede flow.
- Do not rely on unsupported metadata framing or team-assignment claims; those are source-side, not fixable in prose.
- Do not try to build a story from an empty digest or headline-only note alone; if the source has no usable facts, the piece is not writable (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not invent contract length, dollar amount, or other specifics unless they appear in the source digests; strip back to the confirmed agreement when the evidence is thin.
- [insider] Do not add detail the source does not explicitly support, even if the headline summary suggests it.
- [insider] Do not dress a summary-only contract note up with unsupported specifics; if the digest lacks facts, keep the prose minimal.
- [insider] Do not treat a right-player/right-team match as enough on its own; unsupported terms are a sourcing problem, not an execution problem (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not add unconfirmed player mentions or source-framing language when the digest only supports the roster moves themselves (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not imply continuity, centrality, or contract implications that are not actually in the digest; headline-only contract summaries need to stay strictly minimal (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- [insider] Do not keep chasing player-by-player status detail when the source digests are unusable; if the closed world cannot verify the names or statuses, the piece should be dismissed rather than rewritten again (source-side, not fixable in prose).
