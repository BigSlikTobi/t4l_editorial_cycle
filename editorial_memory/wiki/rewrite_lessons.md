# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the story tightly inside the supplied digest and deliver the core update cleanly.
- Lead with the confirmed news peg immediately; don’t bury the main development under setup.
- Use a direct headline and intro that name the concrete development clearly.
- Add only the context that directly strengthens the angle, such as a clear team need or scheme note.
- A narrow insider item works when it stays grounded in the reported status change and relevant background history.
- Preserve a crisp factual sequence: the update, the most relevant history, then the supporting team context.
- Strong grounding can offset a modestly sized update when every added detail is clearly supported.
- Let specific, relevant context do the work instead of broad narrative framing.
- Clean approves are usually the result of restraint: one verified update plus only the context needed to make it make sense.
- [insider] Tight contract updates can publish cleanly when the lede states the confirmed compensation change and nothing else.
- [insider] Use team-specific framing only when it is directly supported and actually helps explain the reported development.
- A blunt, exact quote can carry the piece when the source is thin, as long as the headline and intro stay centered on that verified reaction.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a tightly supported update into broader narrative framing unless the source clearly justifies it.
- Don’t stack extra context that doesn’t directly advance the news peg.
- Don’t let the item drift away from the core status change, visit, or personnel development.
- Don’t repeat the same contract fact in the headline, subhead, and intro without adding new utility.
- Don’t add unsupported claims about role, pecking order, depth chart, or long-term roster certainty.
- Don’t attribute facts to outside reporting or name a reporter/source unless that detail is explicitly in the digest.
- Don’t force a narrow insider note into a bigger strategic story when the digest only supports the immediate update.
- Don’t overcomplicate a clean sourced item with explanation that isn’t necessary for the reader to understand the news.
- Don’t carry over background details unless they sharpen the reported development.
- [insider] Don’t add roster-impact or role conclusions unless the source explicitly supports them.
- [insider] Don’t use team-context details as an excuse to generalize beyond the verified update.
- [insider] Don’t treat a short digest as permission to infer larger organizational implications; stay inside the confirmed facts.
- Don’t build in team-specific framing when the digest only contains a single reaction quote; let the verified quote stand on its own.
- Don’t publish when the digest is effectively empty or missing; there may be no supportable factual base to work from.
- (source-side, not fixable in prose) Don’t try to rescue an article when the source digest is effectively empty; there may be no publishable factual base to work from.
- (source-side, not fixable in prose) Don’t manufacture contract terms, player details, or other specifics that are not present in the available source facts.
- (source-side, not fixable in prose) Don’t proceed as if a missing or placeholder summary were evidence; if the digest lacks facts, the article can’t be grounded.
