# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the piece tightly on the confirmed core development when the digest is thin.
- Lead with the clearest source-backed hook; a single team-specific decision point can carry the story.
- Build the headline and lede around the exact verified angle instead of broadening into summary.
- When the source only supports one clean premise, keep the copy lean and centered on it.
- Direct, specific copy with no fabrication or meta language is a strong default.
- Preserve a clear line from headline to lede so the reader gets the point immediately.
- A narrow factual update can work if it stays fully source-grounded and useful.
- For [analyst] pieces, an open competition or decision-frame can work if it stays inside the verified team context.
- [analyst] Keep evaluation-mode language only when the digest actually supports the names, roles, or details being discussed.
- [analyst] A concise team-specific angle can still read well without extra context if the source package is sparse.

## What to avoid
- Don’t widen a source-backed update into extra narrative, analogy, or implications that the digest does not support.
- Don’t invent player names, actions, or subplots when the source package only confirms the headline-level premise.
- Don’t let unsupported specifics creep into an otherwise usable team angle; strip back to what the digest actually confirms.
- Don’t pad a thin source package with evaluation-mode filler or extra commentary that adds no verified fact.
- Don’t force a broader frame when the real story is just a single competition or decision point.
- Don’t bury the strongest factual takeaway under generic summary language.
- Don’t add timing, significance, or outcome language unless the digest spells it out.
- Don’t overstate certainty or imply consequences the source does not verify.
- Don’t keep roster or personnel references that are not directly supported by the digest.
- Don’t treat missing source detail as a writing problem; if the digest is too thin, stay minimal rather than speculate.
- Don’t try to salvage a piece when the source digest has no factual content beyond a broad premise; that’s source-side, not fixable in prose.
- [analyst] Don’t add camp-rep or depth-chart inference unless the digest explicitly supports those mechanics; otherwise keep the angle at the verified competition frame.
- Don’t present a correction as settled fact when the source material does not verify the underlying detail (source-side, not fixable in prose).
