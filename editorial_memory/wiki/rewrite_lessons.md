# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Keep the headline, lede, and body locked onto one confirmed hook; when one verified development is strong enough, let it carry the item.
- Use a clean, direct news hit when the source is already complete; don’t widen it just to add motion.
- Match the framing to the certainty level of the source; confident sourcing supports a confident but still tightly bounded writeup.
- Keep insider briefs minimal when the source package is thin; one clear fact can be enough.
- Tight, restrained execution helps short items read as finished rather than stitched together.
- Keep headline, intro, and body consistent on the same verified fact; alignment across the item is a clear strength.
- Specific, useful wording for the target audience can work without extra context when the only confirmed fact is clear.
- Strong grounding can outweigh moderate scores elsewhere when the story stays inside one verified update.
- [insider] A narrow practice-report or legal update can work when it stays strictly on the verified status change and nothing more.
- [columnist] A sourced player-specific hook works best when commentary stays out of the lead and the piece remains anchored to the reported development.
- [columnist] A cluster item can still work if it clearly spotlights the one substantial confirmed development and keeps the rest of the mix as brief context.

## What to avoid
- Don’t turn a strong single update into a multi-item cluster recap; that usually dilutes grounding and focus.
- Don’t let the article outrun the source by layering on broader team context or stitched-together side items.
- Don’t make the headline and lede drift into different stories; they should express the same confirmed hook.
- Don’t add roster implications, planning language, or team-wide meaning unless the source explicitly supports them.
- Don’t bury the main fact under secondary references to other people or developments.
- Don’t use broad recap framing when the actual story is one specific transaction or extension.
- Don’t stretch a confirmed deal into larger organizational significance if the source does not supply that meaning.
- Don’t treat a short, source-anchored update as an excuse to pile on extra context that isn’t doing real work.
- Don’t lean on meta language about the source when the digest has no usable facts; if the source is effectively empty, there may be nothing to salvage.
- [insider] Don’t pad a thin update with filler about what the move adds if the digest gives no further particulars.
- [insider] Don’t imply consequences beyond the verified deal or status change; keep the lede tightly on the confirmed news.
- [columnist] Don’t convert a clearly supported player hook into a broader narrative package unless the source truly justifies it.
