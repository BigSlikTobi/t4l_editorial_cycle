# Editorial Style Wiki

## What works (keep doing)
- Open with the single concrete fact the source actually supports, especially when the angle is a comparison or assessment.
- Lead with the clearest verified development first; keep the intro anchored to the named people already in the story.
- Keep the headline and lede on the same verified event, then let the lede add only source-backed detail.
- Use clean, specific framing when the source gives a usable narrow angle rather than trying to broaden it.
- Keep the copy tightly source-bound when the digest is thin; brevity works if every line is factual.
- Let the confirmed development do the work instead of adding explanatory filler or extra interpretation.
- Preserve a direct, factual tone when the source only supports a limited update.
- Strong grounding matters most: if the headline and intro stay inside the verified facts, a short update can still read well.
- [analyst] A tight, team-specific hook can work when it is the cleanest verified angle and the intro stays on that angle.

## What to avoid
- Do not pad a thin update with vague alignment, fallout, or roster language; stay on the concrete verified note.
- Do not widen a narrow comparison or process update into broader meaning, finality, or team posture unless the source directly supports it.
- Do not add interpretive filler or source-narration/meta framing; cut the “report says” style lead when the event itself is the cleaner angle.
- Do not lean on broad commentary when the source only gives one specific comparison, assessment, or timeline detail.
- Do not bring in unrelated broader context just to make a short report feel fuller.
- Do not let headline and intro drift into repetitive restatement without adding a new source-backed detail.
- Do not bury the main verified point under setup or meta language.
- Do not over-explain the implications when the source only supports a single takeaway.
- Do not merge facts from unrelated digests into one narrative, especially when the result creates a wrong-team or wrong-subject claim.
- [analyst] Do not stitch in secondary material that pulls the intro away from the main verified angle.
- [analyst] If the source only offers a broad framing, treat it as source-substance-limited and do not try to manufacture a stronger hook from prose alone.
- Do not invent quotes, player attributions, or camp details when the source digest provides no facts beyond the story title; rebuild from only the verified subject (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not publish when the digest is effectively empty; unsupported comparison-driven rewrites are ungrounded under the closed-world rule (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not introduce unsupported camp-setting specifics or “instant impact” claims when the digest has no usable facts to anchor them (source-side, not fixable in prose).
- Do not force publication from an empty digest; if there is no factual base, the issue is source-side and not salvageable in prose (source-side, not fixable in prose).
